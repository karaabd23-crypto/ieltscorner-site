## Goal
Learn how to use **Present Perfect Continuous** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What Present Perfect Continuous Means
This lesson is specifically about **Present Perfect Continuous**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
Present Perfect Continuous is a tense-aspect control area. You choose forms to place actions on a timeline and show completion, duration, or sequence clearly.

Use Present Perfect Continuous by anchoring time first (past/present/future), then selecting the aspect that matches meaning (simple, progressive, perfect, or perfect progressive).

Use conditions:
- Identify timeline signals (e.g., yesterday, since, by next week) before choosing tense.
- Use perfect forms when linking an earlier action to a later reference point.
- Keep tense shifts intentional and visible when moving between time frames.

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate Present Perfect Continuous helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When Present Perfect Continuous is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Choose the exact grammar job first: where **Present Perfect Continuous** controls time, relationship, or emphasis.
2. Build a complete base clause, then place **Present Perfect Continuous** in the position required by the pattern.
3. Check agreement and tense around **Present Perfect Continuous** before adding extra words.
4. Add one supporting clause only if the logic stays clear in public policy and daily life context.
5. Re-read for one specific risk: wrong form, wrong position, or mixed timeline.

## Real-World Examples with Present Perfect Continuous
### Example 1
- Weak: *I lived here since 2020.*
- Strong: *I have lived here since 2020.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *She has finished her task yesterday.*
- Strong: *She finished her task yesterday.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Present Perfect Continuous
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: mixing present perfect with finished time markers like yesterday</summary>

- Weak: *I lived here since 2020.*
- Strong: *I have lived here since 2020.*
- Fix: use present perfect for unfinished time or life experience.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *She has finished her task yesterday.*
- Strong: *She finished her task yesterday.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in Present Perfect Continuous without improving meaning</summary>

- Weak: *The writer attempts Present Perfect Continuous, but the sentence becomes difficult to follow due to unnecessary additions.*
- Strong: *The writer uses Present Perfect Continuous selectively, so the sentence remains clear and focused.*
- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
</details>
