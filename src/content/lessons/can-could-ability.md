<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Can, Could, Be Able To</strong> are essential modal verbs used to express different degrees of ability and possibility in English. Mastering their usage allows you to convey precise meanings and adapt your language to various contexts and levels of formality.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She can to play the piano well.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She can play the piano well.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Can" is followed directly by the base form of the verb without "to".</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>He could to read by the age of five.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>He could read by the age of five.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Could" is followed directly by the base form of the verb without "to".</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>"Can", "Could", and "Be Able To" are modal verbs used to express ability and possibility. "Can" indicates present ability, "Could" is used for past ability or polite requests, and "Be Able To" is versatile across tenses to show ability. At the B2 level, mastering these forms helps you accurately express different degrees of ability and adapt to various contexts.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing present ability or possibility with "Can".</li>
      <li>Indicating past ability or making polite requests with "Could".</li>
      <li>Using "Be Able To" for future abilities or when a specific tense is needed.</li>
      <li>Formally expressing ability in academic writing or exams.</li>
      <li>Conveying nuanced meanings in professional or formal settings.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>can</strong> speak three languages fluently.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">He <strong>could</strong> swim when he was just four years old.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">By next year, I <strong>will be able to</strong> drive a car.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use "Can" for present ability or informal requests.</li>
      <li>Use "Could" for past ability or polite requests.</li>
      <li>Use "Be Able To" for tense precision across different time frames.</li>
      <li>"Could" is often more polite than "Can" in requests.</li>
      <li>"Be Able To" can replace "Can" or "Could" when specific tense usage is necessary.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using "to" after "can" or "could"</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She can to swim very fast.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She can swim very fast.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Remove "to" after "can" or "could".</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using "be able to" incorrectly</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He was able to can fix the car.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He was able to fix the car.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "be able to" without "can" or "could".</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>confusing "could" with "can" for past ability</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She can read when she was three.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She could read when she was three.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "could" for past abilities.</p>
</article>
</div>
