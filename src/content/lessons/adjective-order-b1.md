## Goal
Learn how to use **Adjective Order** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What Adjective Order Means
This lesson is specifically about **Adjective Order**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
Adjective Order controls how a sentence carries meaning: reference, sequence, and emphasis must stay clear from start to finish.

Use Adjective Order to make one precise meaning per sentence, then expand only when the added structure improves clarity for exam readers.

Use conditions:
- Choose the target form after deciding the exact meaning (fact, contrast, cause, or condition).
- Keep agreement and word order stable before adding extra clauses or modifiers.
- If a longer sentence becomes harder to scan, split it and keep the same logic.

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate Adjective Order helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When Adjective Order is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Choose the exact grammar job first: where **Adjective Order** controls time, relationship, or emphasis.
2. Build a complete base clause, then place **Adjective Order** in the position required by the pattern.
3. Check agreement and tense around **Adjective Order** before adding extra words.
4. Add one supporting clause only if the logic stays clear in public policy and daily life context.
5. Re-read for one specific risk: wrong form, wrong position, or mixed timeline.

## Real-World Examples with Adjective Order
### Example 1
- Weak: *She bought a leather red bag for work.*
- Strong: *She bought a red leather bag for work.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *They live in a house modern large near downtown.*
- Strong: *They live in a large modern house near downtown.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Adjective Order
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: placing adjectives in a random order before the noun</summary>

- Weak: *She bought a leather red bag for work.*
- Strong: *She bought a red leather bag for work.*
- Fix: follow a natural sequence such as opinion, size, age, shape, color, origin, material.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *They live in a house modern large near downtown.*
- Strong: *They live in a large modern house near downtown.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in Adjective Order without improving meaning</summary>

- Weak: *The writer attempts Adjective Order, but the sentence becomes difficult to follow due to unnecessary additions.*
- Strong: *The writer uses Adjective Order selectively, so the sentence remains clear and focused.*
- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
</details>
