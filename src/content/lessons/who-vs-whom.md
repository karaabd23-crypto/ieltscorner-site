<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Who vs Whom</strong> helps you distinguish between subjects and objects in a sentence, enhancing clarity and precision. This lesson will guide you in using these pronouns correctly in various contexts.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span> Whom is going to the meeting?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span> Who is going to the meeting?</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Who' is the correct choice because it is the subject of the verb 'is going'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span> Who did you speak with?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span> Whom did you speak with?</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Whom' is correct here because it is the object of the preposition 'with'.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>'Who' and 'whom' are pronouns used to refer to people. 'Who' is used as a subject, while 'whom' is used as an object in a sentence.</p>
    <p>Understanding the difference helps in crafting grammatically correct sentences, especially in formal writing and speech.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering 'who' and 'whom' enhances clarity and professionalism in communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Using 'who' as the subject of a verb (e.g., "Who is coming?").</li>
      <li>Using 'whom' as the object of a verb or preposition (e.g., "To whom should I send this?").</li>
      <li>Formulating questions or relative clauses that require precision in formal contexts.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Who</strong> is responsible for this project?</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The person <strong>whom</strong> you met yesterday is my colleague.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'who' when referring to the subject of a clause.</li>
      <li>Use 'whom' when referring to the object of a verb or preposition.</li>
      <li>In formal writing, always ensure the correct use of 'who' and 'whom' to maintain clarity.</li>
      <li>Remember: 'who' = subject, 'whom' = object.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using 'who' as an object</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> Who should I ask for assistance?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> Whom should I ask for assistance?</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'whom' when it is the object of the verb 'ask'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using 'whom' as a subject</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> Whom is responsible for this task?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> Who is responsible for this task?</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'who' when it is the subject of the verb 'is'.</p>
</article>
</div>
