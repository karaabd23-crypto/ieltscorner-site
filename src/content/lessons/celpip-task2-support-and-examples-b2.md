## Topic Explanation and Use
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Core idea</p>
    <p>In CELPIP Task 2, a clear opinion is only the start. The score improves when each reason is developed enough for the reader to trust it.</p>
    <p>Strong support usually has three simple parts. First, give the reason. Second, explain why that reason matters. Third, add one realistic example or consequence. This is enough. You do not need a long story.</p>
    <p>Many students lose marks because they stay too general. They write sentences like <em>this is better for people</em> or <em>this option has many advantages</em>. These ideas are not wrong, but they are too thin. The reader still asks, <em>Better for who? In what way? What would happen?</em></p>
    <p>At B2 level, support becomes stronger when you choose one clear benefit and develop it fully instead of listing many weak benefits quickly.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it here</p>
    <ul><li>State your choice early, then build one reason at a time.</li><li>After each reason, explain the result for real people, time, cost, convenience, or stress.</li><li>Add one short example that sounds possible in everyday life.</li></ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Watch it work</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack"><div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I would choose flexible work hours because they help employees manage family duties more easily.</div>
<div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">For example, parents can start earlier, finish earlier, and avoid expensive after-school child care.</div></div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Remember this</p>
    <ul><li>One strong reason is better than three weak reasons.</li><li>Ask yourself: why does this reason matter in real life?</li><li>Keep examples short and believable.</li><li>Do not repeat the same opinion using different words.</li><li>Link the example back to the main choice.</li></ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Errors with CELPIP Task 2: Support and Examples
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>giving a reason without explaining why it matters</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This plan is better because it is more convenient.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>This plan is better because it reduces commuting time, which gives workers more time for family responsibilities in the evening.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> After the reason, add one clear result.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using examples that are too broad</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Many people can benefit from this.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>For example, new immigrants can attend evening classes after work instead of losing income during the day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Name the people and the concrete benefit.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>repeating the opinion instead of developing it</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>I think this option is best because it is the best choice for people.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>I think this option is best because it lowers monthly costs and gives families a more stable schedule.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Replace repeated opinion language with a real benefit.</p>
</article>
</div>
