Respond to a Situation asks you to speak for a practical purpose. You might need to apologize, ask for help, give advice, or leave a message. The task is not about sounding dramatic. It is about sounding clear, polite, and complete in a real-life situation.

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Main idea</p>
  <p>Most situation responses can use a four-part frame: greeting, situation, action, closing. The action may be a request, apology, explanation, offer, or suggestion.</p>
  <p>Tone matters. If you are speaking to a manager, teacher, service worker, or neighbour, use polite language. If you are speaking to a friend, sound warm but still clear.</p>
  <p>Do not add a long story. The listener needs to know what happened and what should happen next. Specific details are useful only when they support that purpose.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Score-safe habits</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Identify who you are speaking to.</li>
      <li>Name the problem or purpose quickly.</li>
      <li>Use one clear request, offer, or suggestion.</li>
      <li>Close politely so the response feels complete.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">No clear action</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>I have an issue with the laptop and it is confusing.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Could you please tell me whether I should return it today or visit the help desk first?</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> The listener needs the next step.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Wrong tone</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Fix this for me now.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Could you please help me resolve this today?</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Polite tone fits most practical prompts.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Too much background</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Yesterday I was busy and then I walked to many places before I noticed the problem.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>I noticed the charger was missing when I opened the laptop bag.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Keep only useful context.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
PTE Core is scored through computer-delivered tasks, but the human skill underneath is still clear communication. When you understand Respond to a Situation structure, you waste less time guessing what the task wants. You can focus on meaning, structure, grammar, pronunciation, or listening accuracy in the exact way the task requires. This makes practice more efficient and makes the simulator feel less intimidating.
