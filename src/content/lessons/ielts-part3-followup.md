<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>IELTS Speaking Part 3: Follow-up Questions</strong> is a speaking skill. This lesson shows how to answer clearly, add support, and keep your response easy to follow even when you feel pressure.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>I think this idea is good for many reasons.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I support investing more in public transport because it helps a larger number of people every day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The better answer stays on the same idea but starts doing the real speaking job immediately.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>For example, it helps people and makes things better.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>For example, better bus service can reduce commuting stress for workers who do not own cars.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger sentence keeps the same function but adds meaning instead of filler.</p>
</article>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>In IELTS Speaking Part 3, follow-up questions require you to expand on your ideas with clarity and depth. This involves stating your opinion clearly, supporting it with reasons, and providing examples to illustrate your point. Mastering this skill helps you maintain coherence and fluency under exam conditions.</p>
    <p>For B2 level speakers, this means organizing thoughts quickly and expressing them in a structured manner to maximize your speaking score.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Answering follow-up questions that require detailed explanations.</li>
      <li>Providing examples to support your opinion in a discussion.</li>
      <li>Structuring your response to maintain coherence and fluency.</li>
      <li>Transitioning between ideas smoothly during the speaking test.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>I believe</strong> investing more in public transport is essential because it <strong>benefits</strong> a larger number of people daily.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>For instance</strong>, improved bus services can <strong>reduce</strong> commuting stress for workers without cars.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Start with a clear statement of your opinion.</li>
      <li>Provide one strong reason to support your opinion.</li>
      <li>Use specific examples to illustrate your point.</li>
      <li>Conclude with a summary or broader comment if time allows.</li>
      <li>Avoid fillers; focus on clarity and coherence.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>postponing the opinion</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This topic has many sides, so it is difficult to answer immediately.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>I support more public transport investment because it helps more residents on a daily basis.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> State the opinion in the first sentence.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using an empty supporting example</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>For example, it helps people and improves things in society.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>For instance, frequent bus service helps shift workers reach jobs outside the city center.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Choose one specific consequence or case.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>closing without a clear position</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>So yes, there are many reasons for that idea.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Overall, that is why I see transport funding as the more practical policy choice.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> End by restating your position in one line.</p>
</article>
</div>
