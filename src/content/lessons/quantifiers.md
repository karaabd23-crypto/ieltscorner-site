<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Quantifiers and Amounts</strong> focus on the vocabulary needed to accurately describe quantities and amounts in English. This lesson explains the meaning of key quantifiers, their appropriate contexts, and how to use them naturally in sentences, enhancing both clarity and precision in communication.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>There are much reasons to book early in summer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>There are many reasons to book early in summer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Many' is used with countable nouns like 'reasons'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>We have a few time before the shop closes.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>We have a little time before the shop closes.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'A little' is appropriate for uncountable nouns like 'time'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 3</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>She has few friends in the city.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She has a few friends in the city.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'A few' indicates a positive number of countable nouns like 'friends'.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Quantifiers are words or phrases used to express the quantity of something. They help specify amounts precisely, whether dealing with countable or uncountable nouns. At the B2 level, understanding and using quantifiers accurately enhances clarity and precision in both spoken and written communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing quantities in academic writing or reports.</li>
      <li>Discussing time, money, or other measurable concepts in formal contexts.</li>
      <li>Clarifying amounts in instructions or guidelines.</li>
      <li>Expressing frequency or degree in everyday conversation.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">There are <strong>several</strong> options available for the project.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">We need <strong>a little</strong> more time to complete the task.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She has <strong>enough</strong> experience to lead the team.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'many' with countable nouns and 'much' with uncountable nouns.</li>
      <li>'A few' and 'a little' imply a positive amount, while 'few' and 'little' suggest scarcity.</li>
      <li>Always pair quantifiers with the correct noun type to maintain clarity.</li>
      <li>Consider the context to choose the most precise quantifier.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using a countable quantifier with an uncountable noun</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We do not have many information about the delay.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We do not have much information about the delay.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> match 'many'/'few' with countable nouns and 'much'/'little' with uncountable nouns.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using few or little when the intended meaning is positive</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Few minutes remain, so we can still finish comfortably.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>A few minutes remain, so we can still finish comfortably.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> use 'a few' or 'a little' when you mean some, not almost none.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>memorizing the quantifier without the noun type</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>There was many traffic near the station.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>There was heavy traffic near the station.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> learn the noun type with the quantifier so the phrase stays natural.</p>
</article>
</div>
