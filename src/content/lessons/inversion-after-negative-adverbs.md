<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Inversion After Negative Words</strong> is a grammar topic that involves changing the usual word order to emphasize certain parts of a sentence. This lesson will help you understand how to correctly use inversion after negative adverbs, see clear examples, and avoid common mistakes.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Never I have seen such a clear explanation.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span><strong>Never have</strong> I seen such a clear explanation.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Inversion is used after "never" to emphasize the rarity of the event.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Rarely we consider the long-term effects.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span><strong>Rarely do</strong> we consider the long-term effects.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The auxiliary "do" is placed before the subject to form the correct inversion.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Inversion after negative words involves changing the usual subject-verb order to verb-subject order to emphasize a negative adverb or adverbial phrase at the beginning of a sentence. This structure is used to add emphasis and formality to statements.</p>
    <p>The pattern typically follows: <strong>Negative word/phrase + auxiliary + subject + main verb</strong>.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering this structure helps increase sophistication in both writing and speaking.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Emphasizing a negative condition or situation.</li>
      <li>Beginning a sentence with negative adverbs like "never," "rarely," or "seldom."</li>
      <li>Writing formal texts, such as essays or reports, where emphasis is needed.</li>
      <li>Making a point more dramatic in storytelling or speeches.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Never have</strong> I seen such a beautiful sunset.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Seldom does</strong> he visit his hometown.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use inversion after negative adverbs at the start of a sentence.</li>
      <li>Place the auxiliary verb before the subject.</li>
      <li>Ensure the main verb follows the subject.</li>
      <li>Remember that not all negative words trigger inversion, only those at the beginning of a sentence.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Incorrect inversion structure</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Never I have seen such dedication.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span><strong>Never have</strong> I seen such dedication.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Place the auxiliary "have" before the subject "I" after the negative adverb "never."</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Omitting the auxiliary verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Seldom he visits his family.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span><strong>Seldom does</strong> he visit his family.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "does" before the subject "he" to maintain correct inversion.</p>
</article>
</div>
