## Goal
Learn how to use **Word Order After Negative Words** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What Word Order After Negative Words Means
This lesson is specifically about **Word Order After Negative Words**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
Word Order After Negative Words controls how a sentence carries meaning: reference, sequence, and emphasis must stay clear from start to finish.

Use Word Order After Negative Words to make one precise meaning per sentence, then expand only when the added structure improves clarity for exam readers.

Use conditions:
- Choose the target form after deciding the exact meaning (fact, contrast, cause, or condition).
- Keep agreement and word order stable before adding extra clauses or modifiers.
- If a longer sentence becomes harder to scan, split it and keep the same logic.

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate Word Order After Negative Words helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When Word Order After Negative Words is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Choose the exact grammar job first: where **Word Order After Negative Words** controls time, relationship, or emphasis.
2. Build a complete base clause, then place **Word Order After Negative Words** in the position required by the pattern.
3. Check agreement and tense around **Word Order After Negative Words** before adding extra words.
4. Add one supporting clause only if the logic stays clear in public policy and daily life context.
5. Re-read for one specific risk: wrong form, wrong position, or mixed timeline.

## Real-World Examples with Word Order After Negative Words
### Example 1
- Weak: *Why you are late for class today?*
- Strong: *Why are you late for class today?*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *Never I have seen such a clear explanation.*
- Strong: *Never have I seen such a clear explanation.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Word Order After Negative Words
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: placing auxiliaries and subjects in the wrong sequence</summary>

- Weak: *Why you are late for class today?*
- Strong: *Why are you late for class today?*
- Fix: check whether your sentence requires statement order or inversion.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *Never I have seen such a clear explanation.*
- Strong: *Never have I seen such a clear explanation.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in Word Order After Negative Words without improving meaning</summary>

- Weak: *The writer attempts Word Order After Negative Words, but the sentence becomes difficult to follow due to unnecessary additions.*
- Strong: *The writer uses Word Order After Negative Words selectively, so the sentence remains clear and focused.*
- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
</details>
