<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Phrasal Verbs: Movement</strong> focuses on verbs that describe movement and direction. This lesson helps you understand their meanings, correct usage, and how to incorporate them into your speech and writing for more natural English.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>We started the journey at dawn.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>We <strong>set off</strong> at dawn.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger version uses a phrasal verb to convey the start of a journey more naturally.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>He climbed the ladder quickly.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>He <strong>climbed up</strong> the ladder quickly.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The better sentence uses 'climbed up' to specify the direction of movement.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Phrasal verbs related to movement combine a verb with a particle to express actions involving direction or physical movement. These verbs often convey a specific nuance that a single verb might not capture.</p>
    <p>For example, 'set off' means to begin a journey. At the B2 level, understanding these nuances helps in expressing complex ideas more naturally.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing the start, continuation, or end of a journey or movement.</li>
      <li>Explaining how something moves or changes location.</li>
      <li>Discussing actions that involve direction or physical movement, such as in travel or sports contexts.</li>
      <li>Writing narratives or stories where movement is a key element.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">We <strong>set off</strong> for the airport early in the morning.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The cat <strong>jumped over</strong> the fence to chase the bird.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Learn phrasal verbs as fixed expressions to understand their full meaning.</li>
      <li>Use phrasal verbs to add precision and naturalness to descriptions of movement.</li>
      <li>Check if the object can be placed between the verb and the particle.</li>
      <li>Be mindful of the context to ensure the phrasal verb fits the situation.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>choosing the wrong particle with a familiar verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The hikers set the mountain off early.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The hikers <strong>set off</strong> early for the mountain.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> learn the full phrasal verb as one item</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>placing the object in an unnatural position</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She jumped the fence over to catch the ball.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She <strong>jumped over</strong> the fence to catch the ball.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> check where the object belongs in the phrase</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using a phrasal verb without understanding its meaning</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The car ran out of the road.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The car <strong>ran off</strong> the road.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> use the phrasal verb only when you know what action it describes</p>
</article>
</div>
