<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Noun Clauses With That and Whether</strong> are essential for constructing complex sentences that convey precise meanings. This lesson will help you understand how to use these clauses effectively, see clear examples, and avoid common mistakes.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>I believe whether the program needs more funding.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I believe <strong>that</strong> the program needs more funding.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use <strong>that</strong> to report a statement or belief, not <strong>whether</strong>.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The committee has not decided if the centre should stay open.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The committee has not decided <strong>whether</strong> the centre should stay open.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use <strong>whether</strong> for expressing yes/no questions or uncertainty in formal contexts.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Noun clauses are dependent clauses that function as nouns within a sentence. They often begin with words like <strong>that</strong> or <strong>whether</strong> and can express statements, questions, or uncertainty.</p>
    <p>These clauses are used to report information, beliefs, or decisions without asking direct questions. At the B2 level, mastering noun clauses helps in constructing complex sentences for clearer communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Reporting information or beliefs, using <strong>that</strong>.</li>
      <li>Expressing uncertainty or yes/no questions, using <strong>whether</strong>.</li>
      <li>After verbs like think, believe, decide, or wonder.</li>
      <li>In formal writing and speaking, to convey precise meanings.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She explained <strong>that</strong> the meeting was postponed.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They are unsure <strong>whether</strong> the event will occur.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>that</strong> to report statements or beliefs.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>whether</strong> for yes/no questions or uncertainty.</li>
      <li>Keep the noun clause in statement order, not question order.</li>
      <li>Ensure verb agreement in the main clause.</li>
      <li>Choose the clause opener based on the meaning you want to convey.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Choosing the wrong noun-clause opener</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The teacher explained if the deadline had changed.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The teacher explained <strong>that</strong> the deadline had changed.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use <strong>that</strong> for reporting information.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using 'if' where formal English needs 'whether'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The board discussed if the branch should stay open.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The board discussed <strong>whether</strong> the branch should stay open.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use <strong>whether</strong> for yes/no choices in formal writing.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Adding question order after a reporting verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She asked what did the manager decide.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She asked what the manager decided.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Keep statement order inside the noun clause.</p>
</article>
</div>
