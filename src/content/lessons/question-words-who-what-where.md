<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Question words</strong> like 'who,' 'what,' and 'where' are essential for asking questions in English. They help you gather specific information about people, objects, and places, making your communication clearer and more effective. This lesson will guide you through the specific use of these words in forming questions, provide examples, and highlight common mistakes to avoid.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Who you think is the best candidate?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Who do you think is the best candidate?</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use 'do' to form the question correctly when 'who' is not the subject.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Where you bought this book?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Where did you buy this book?</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use 'did' to form the question correctly in the past tense.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>'Who,' 'what,' and 'where' are question words used to gather specific information about people, things, and places in English. They initiate questions that require specific answers rather than yes/no responses. Mastery of these question forms is crucial at the B2 level for effective communication and clarity in both spoken and written English.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Asking about a person's identity or role, use 'who.'</li>
      <li>Inquiring about objects or actions, use 'what.'</li>
      <li>Seeking information about locations, use 'where.'</li>
      <li>Formulating questions in formal writing, interviews, or exams.</li>
      <li>Clarifying details in conversations or discussions.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Who</strong> is responsible for this project?</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>What</strong> are the main objectives of the meeting?</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Where</strong> did you find this information?</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Start questions with 'who,' 'what,' or 'where' when seeking specific information.</li>
      <li>Follow the question word with an auxiliary verb if needed (do, does, did).</li>
      <li>Ensure the word order is correct: question word + auxiliary + subject + main verb.</li>
      <li>Do not use an auxiliary if the question word is the subject.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Incorrect word order with 'who'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Who you think will win?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Who do you think will win?</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'do' to form the question when 'who' is not the subject.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Missing auxiliary with 'what'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>What you want to achieve?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>What do you want to achieve?</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Add 'do' to form the question correctly.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Incorrect word order with 'where'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Where you found this document?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Where did you find this document?</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'did' to form the question correctly in the past tense.</p>
</article>
</div>
