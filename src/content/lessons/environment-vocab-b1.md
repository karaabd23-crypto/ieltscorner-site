## Topic Explanation and Use
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Core idea</p>
    <p>This lesson helps you say the same idea with sharper, more natural vocabulary.</p>
<p>Environment and Ecology teaches the words you need to describe pollution, sustainability, conservation, and energy choices clearly.</p>
<p>Use this vocabulary when discussing environmental causes, effects, and solutions in writing and speaking.</p>
<p>At B1 level, learn fewer words but learn them well: meaning, collocation, and one model sentence.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it here</p>
    <ul><li>Choose words that match the exact issue, such as emissions, air quality, or renewable energy.</li><li>Pair the topic word with a realistic cause, effect, or solution sentence.</li><li>Avoid broad words like problem when a more exact environmental term is available.</li></ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Watch it work</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack"><div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Lower vehicle emissions improved air quality near the school.</div>
<div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Investment in renewable energy supports long-term sustainability.</div></div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Remember this</p>
    <ul><li>Choose the meaning first before you choose the word.</li><li>Learn the word with a natural collocation or partner phrase.</li><li>Use the new word in one short sentence right away.</li><li>Replace vague words only when the new word stays accurate.</li><li>Keep the register stable so the language fits the task.</li></ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Word Bank and Useful Chunks
<div class="lesson-resource-grid">
  <article class="lesson-resource-card">
    <p class="lesson-card-label">Word bank</p>
    <ul><li>emissions: gases released into the air</li><li>renewable energy: power from naturally replaced sources</li><li>waste management: systems for handling waste</li><li>air quality: how clean the air is</li><li>conservation: protection of natural resources</li><li>sustainability: meeting needs without long-term damage</li></ul>
  </article>
  <article class="lesson-resource-card">

## Common Errors with Environment and Ecology
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using broad environmental words instead of the exact issue</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The city solved the pollution problem.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The city reduced vehicle emissions in the centre.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> name the exact environmental issue directly</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using a topic word without cause, effect, or solution</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Air quality is important.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Air quality improved after diesel buses were replaced.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> attach the term to a real cause or result</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>mixing environmental terms loosely</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Renewable energy made conservation in the traffic system.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Renewable energy reduced the city&#39;s reliance on fossil fuels.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> use the topic word in a sentence that matches its real meaning</p>
</article>
</div>
