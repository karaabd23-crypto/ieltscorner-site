<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Perfect vs Simple Tenses</strong> helps you express <em>time relationships</em> clearly. Choosing the right tense allows you to indicate when actions occur and their connection to other events. In this lesson, you'll learn how to match tense forms to their meanings effectively.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>By the time you arrive, I finish my work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>By the time you arrive, I will have finished my work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Will have finished' is used to show the action will be completed before another future event.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She reads every morning before work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She reads every morning before work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The simple present 'reads' correctly describes a habitual action.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Perfect tenses (present, past, future) express actions that are completed in relation to another time. Simple tenses (present, past, future) describe actions that are habitual, general truths, or completed at a specific time.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering these tenses helps you clarify when actions occur and their relationship to other events.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing an action completed before another in the past (past perfect).</li>
      <li>Indicating an action that will be completed before a future time (future perfect).</li>
      <li>Talking about habits or general truths (simple present).</li>
      <li>Describing a completed action at a specific past time (simple past).</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">By the time you arrive, I <strong>will have finished</strong> my work.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>reads</strong> every morning before work.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use perfect tenses to show completed actions in relation to another time.</li>
      <li>Use simple tenses for habits, general truths, or specific completed actions.</li>
      <li>Ensure the verb tense matches the time marker in the sentence.</li>
      <li>Avoid unnecessary tense shifts within a sentence unless the timeline changes.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using present perfect with a finished time marker</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She has finished the report yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She finished the report yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use simple past with finished time markers like yesterday or last week.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Switching tense without a time reason</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The study started in May and shows strong results in June.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The study started in May and showed strong results in June.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Keep the tense stable unless the timeline genuinely changes.</p>
</article>
</div>
