<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>A, An, The</strong> are articles used before nouns to indicate specificity: <em>a</em>, <em>an</em>, and <em>the</em>. They help convey whether something is general, one of many, or already known. In this lesson, you will learn how to use each article correctly to enhance clarity and precision in your writing.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>I bought book and umbrella from market.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I bought <strong>a</strong> book and <strong>an</strong> umbrella from <strong>the</strong> market.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use <strong>a</strong> or <strong>an</strong> for singular, non-specific nouns, and <strong>the</strong> for specific, known nouns.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Sun rises in east every day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span><strong>The</strong> sun rises in <strong>the</strong> east every day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use <strong>the</strong> for unique nouns and known directions.</p>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Articles are words that precede nouns to indicate their specificity. They help clarify whether a noun is general, specific, known, or unknown to the reader. The English articles are <strong>a</strong>, <strong>an</strong>, and <strong>the</strong>. At the B2 level, mastering articles enhances clarity and precision in communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Introducing a singular, countable noun not previously mentioned, use <strong>a</strong> or <strong>an</strong>.</li>
      <li>Referring to a specific noun known to both the speaker and listener, use <strong>the</strong>.</li>
      <li>Making general statements about plural or uncountable nouns, omit the article.</li>
      <li>Talking about unique nouns, like <strong>the sun</strong> or <strong>the moon</strong>.</li>
      <li>Using <strong>a</strong> or <strong>an</strong> based on the sound of the following word, not the spelling.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>A</strong> cat sat on <strong>the</strong> mat.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>An</strong> apple a day keeps <strong>the</strong> doctor away.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>a</strong> before words starting with a consonant sound, and <strong>an</strong> before vowel sounds.</li>
      <li>Always use <strong>the</strong> for specific nouns known to both speaker and listener.</li>
      <li>Do not use an article with plural or uncountable nouns when speaking generally.</li>
      <li>Remember that <strong>the</strong> is used for unique objects or concepts.</li>
      <li>Check if the noun is new or known information to decide on the article.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Omitting <strong>a</strong>, <strong>an</strong>, or <strong>the</strong> where a noun needs one</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She wants to become engineer in future.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She wants to become <strong>an</strong> engineer in <strong>the</strong> future.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Check singular countable nouns and known specific references.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using <strong>the</strong> with a general plural noun</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span><strong>The</strong> bicycles are healthier than driving in cities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Bicycles are healthier than driving in cities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Remove <strong>the</strong> when you mean things in general.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Choosing <strong>a</strong>/<strong>an</strong> from spelling instead of sound</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He waited for <strong>a</strong> hour before boarding.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He waited for <strong>an</strong> hour before boarding.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Choose <strong>a</strong>/<strong>an</strong> from sound, not the first letter.</p>
</article>
</div>
