<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Few, A Few, Little, A Little</strong> helps you talk about number and amount clearly. English changes the form depending on whether the noun is countable, uncountable, small in number, or large in amount. In this lesson, you will learn how to choose the right quantity word for the noun.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>There are few milk in the fridge.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>There is little milk in the fridge.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Milk" is uncountable, so "little" is the correct quantifier.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>He has a little friends in the city.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>He has a few friends in the city.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Friends" is countable, so "a few" is the correct quantifier.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>"Few" and "a few" are used with countable nouns, while "little" and "a little" are used with uncountable nouns. "Few" and "little" imply a smaller quantity than desired, whereas "a few" and "a little" suggest a small but sufficient amount. This distinction helps convey precise meaning in sentences, particularly at the B2 level where nuanced language use is important for clarity and accuracy.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing a small quantity of countable nouns with "few" or "a few".</li>
      <li>Indicating a small amount of uncountable nouns with "little" or "a little".</li>
      <li>Expressing insufficiency with "few" or "little".</li>
      <li>Conveying adequacy with "a few" or "a little".</li>
      <li>Writing essays or reports where precise quantity is important.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">There are <strong>few</strong> opportunities to advance in this company.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She has <strong>a little</strong> time to spare before her meeting.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Only <strong>a few</strong> people attended the workshop.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use "few" and "a few" with plural countable nouns.</li>
      <li>Use "little" and "a little" with uncountable nouns.</li>
      <li>"Few" and "little" imply insufficiency; "a few" and "a little" imply sufficiency.</li>
      <li>Be mindful of the subtle difference in meaning to convey the right tone.</li>
      <li>Ensure the noun type matches the quantifier used.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Mixing countable and uncountable quantifiers</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We have few information about the event.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We have little information about the event.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "little" with uncountable nouns like "information".</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using "few" or "little" when the meaning is positive</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>There are few people who liked the presentation.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>There are a few people who liked the presentation.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "a few" to indicate a positive or sufficient amount.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Confusing "a little" with "little" for positive meaning</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We have little hope of success.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We have a little hope of success.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "a little" to express a small but sufficient amount.</p>
</article>
</div>
