## Goal
Learn how to use **First conditional** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What First conditional Means
This lesson is specifically about **First conditional**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
First conditional controls hypothetical vs real situations. The form shows whether a result is factual, probable, or imagined.

Use First conditional by matching the if-clause form to the result clause so probability and timeline stay consistent.

Use conditions:
- Choose one conditional pattern and keep both clauses aligned.
- Use would/could/might in result clauses for unreal present/future meaning.
- Avoid mixing real and unreal patterns in one condition-result pair.

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate First conditional helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When First conditional is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Choose the exact grammar job first: where **First conditional** controls time, relationship, or emphasis.
2. Build a complete base clause, then place **First conditional** in the position required by the pattern.
3. Check agreement and tense around **First conditional** before adding extra words.
4. Add one supporting clause only if the logic stays clear in public policy and daily life context.
5. Re-read for one specific risk: wrong form, wrong position, or mixed timeline.

## Real-World Examples with First conditional
### Example 1
- Weak: *If governments invest in transit, traffic reduced quickly.*
- Strong: *If governments invest in transit, traffic will reduce quickly.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *If I knew the answer, I will tell you now.*
- Strong: *If I knew the answer, I would tell you now.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with First conditional
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: mixing first and second conditional forms in one sentence</summary>

- Weak: *If governments invest in transit, traffic reduced quickly.*
- Strong: *If governments invest in transit, traffic will reduce quickly.*
- Fix: match the if-clause tense and result clause form correctly.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *If I knew the answer, I will tell you now.*
- Strong: *If I knew the answer, I would tell you now.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in First conditional without improving meaning</summary>

- Weak: *The writer attempts First conditional, but the sentence becomes difficult to follow due to unnecessary additions.*
- Strong: *The writer uses First conditional selectively, so the sentence remains clear and focused.*
- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
</details>
