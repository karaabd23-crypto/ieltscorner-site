<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Formal vs Informal Writing</strong> is a key skill for IELTS and CELPIP. This lesson helps you understand when to use each style, how to craft a strong response, and what to check before submitting your work. Mastering this skill will enhance your ability to communicate effectively in both exams and real-world scenarios.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Informal</span>Hey, just wanted to say thanks for the invite!</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Formal</span>I would like to express my gratitude for the invitation.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The formal version uses polite and structured language suitable for professional settings.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Informal</span>Can you give me a hand with this?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Formal</span>Could you please assist me with this task?</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The formal version is more polite and appropriate for professional requests.</p>
</article>
</div>


## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Formal writing uses structured, objective, and precise language, often necessary for academic and professional contexts. Informal writing is more relaxed and conversational, suitable for casual communication. Mastering both styles is crucial for IELTS and CELPIP, as these exams assess your ability to adapt writing to different contexts and audiences.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Writing essays, reports, or formal letters in exams or professional settings.</li>
      <li>Crafting emails or messages where a conversational tone is more appropriate.</li>
      <li>Responding to IELTS or CELPIP tasks that specify a required tone.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Formal:</strong> I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with the service I received.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Informal:</strong> Hey, just wanted to let you know I'm not happy with the service.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use formal language for academic and professional settings.</li>
      <li>Informal language is suitable for personal and casual communication.</li>
      <li>Avoid contractions and slang in formal writing.</li>
      <li>Adapt your tone to match the audience and purpose of your writing.</li>
      <li>Maintain consistency in tone throughout your writing.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using informal contractions in a formal essay</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>It's clear that the government can't ignore this issue.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>It is clear that the government cannot ignore this issue.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Expand all contractions (it's → it is, can't → cannot) in formal academic writing.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using conversational filler phrases in a formal letter</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>I just wanted to let you know that I am kind of unhappy with the service.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>I am writing to express my dissatisfaction with the service I received.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Replace "just", "kind of", "sort of", and "wanted to" with precise, formal verbs.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Switching between formal and informal register in the same response</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The policy has numerous benefits. But honestly, who's going to follow it?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The policy has numerous benefits; however, enforcement remains a significant challenge.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Choose one register and maintain it throughout. Formal writing avoids rhetorical questions.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters

This skill is also invaluable in professional and personal communication outside of exams.</p>
