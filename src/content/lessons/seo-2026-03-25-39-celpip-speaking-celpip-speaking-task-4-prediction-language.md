<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">CELPIP Speaking Task 4 asks you to make predictions about a future situation based on the information provided. Accurate modal usage and evidence-linking are the two markers of a CLB 9 response.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Evidence-based prediction</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Link your prediction explicitly to the data or scenario given</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"Given the steady rise in applicants since 2022, demand will likely exceed capacity within two years."</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Graded certainty</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Use "will", "is likely to", "may", "could" to show confidence level</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"Sales will increase" (certain); "Sales are likely to increase" (probable); "Sales may increase" (possible)</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Quantity or timeframe</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Add a specific number or timeframe to anchor the prediction</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"Ridership is expected to rise by 20% within 18 months of the station opening."</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Consequence chain</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Extend the prediction with a second-order consequence</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"As demand increases, prices will rise, which may reduce affordability for first-time buyers."</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Prediction without evidence</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I think the company will do well in the future.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Given the 40% year-over-year growth in online sales, the company is likely to expand its warehouse capacity within 12 months.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Every prediction should name the evidence that supports it.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Flat certainty</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Everything will definitely be fine.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Revenue will probably recover by Q3, assuming the supply chain disruptions resolve as expected.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Absolute certainty about future events reduces credibility. Use "probably", "likely", "is expected to".</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">No timeframe</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>The situation will improve.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The situation is expected to stabilise within six months as the new policy takes effect.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Add a timeframe ("within six months", "by 2028") to make the prediction specific and evaluable.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Speaking Task 4 tests your ability to interpret a situation and project it forward. The examiner is looking for evidence-based reasoning, appropriate modal usage, and specificity. Candidates who say "I think things will improve" without evidence or a timeframe score CLB 7 on Task Fulfilment. Adding one evidence link, one graded modal, and one timeframe converts a vague prediction into a CLB 9 response.</p>
