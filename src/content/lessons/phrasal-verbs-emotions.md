<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Phrasal Verbs for Emotions</strong> focuses on enhancing your ability to express feelings and emotional reactions naturally in English. Mastering these verbs will help you communicate more effectively in both spoken and written contexts, allowing you to convey emotions vividly and accurately.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>She became happy after hearing the news.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She <strong>cheered up</strong> after hearing the news.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger version uses a phrasal verb to express the change in emotion more vividly.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>He was very scared when he saw the snake.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>He <strong>freaked out</strong> when he saw the snake.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Using "freaked out" conveys a stronger emotional reaction.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Phrasal verbs for emotions are combinations of verbs and particles that convey specific emotional states or reactions. They allow for more nuanced expression in English.</p>
    <p>These verbs often involve a verb followed by a preposition or adverb, such as "cheer up" or "calm down." Understanding these combinations is crucial at the B2 level to express emotions naturally in both spoken and written English.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing a change in emotional state, e.g., "cheer up" when someone becomes happier.</li>
      <li>Expressing emotional reactions to events, e.g., "freak out" when surprised or scared.</li>
      <li>Giving advice or commands related to emotions, e.g., "calm down" when someone is upset.</li>
      <li>Writing informal emails or messages where emotional expression is key.</li>
      <li>In spoken English, to make conversations sound more natural and fluent.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">After the good news, she <strong>cheered up</strong> significantly.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">He <strong>freaked out</strong> when he saw the spider.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">You need to <strong>calm down</strong> before we can discuss this.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Learn phrasal verbs as fixed expressions to ensure correct usage.</li>
      <li>Use the correct particle to convey the intended emotion.</li>
      <li>Consider the context; some phrasal verbs may be too informal for certain situations.</li>
      <li>Practice using phrasal verbs in sentences to solidify understanding.</li>
      <li>Be aware of phrasal verbs that have multiple meanings depending on context.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>choosing the wrong particle with a familiar verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She cheered of after the exam results.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She <strong>cheered up</strong> after the exam results.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> learn the full phrasal verb as one item</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>placing the object in an unnatural position</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Please calm yourself down before we talk.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Please <strong>calm down</strong> before we talk.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> check where the object belongs in the phrase</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using a phrasal verb without understanding its meaning</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He calmed up when he saw the mess.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He <strong>calmed down</strong> after realizing it wasn't a big deal.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> use the phrasal verb only when you know what action it describes</p>
</article>
</div>
