<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Past Continuous</strong> helps you depict actions that were ongoing at a specific time in the past. This tense is crucial for narrating stories, describing scenes, and explaining past events in detail. In this lesson, you will learn how to effectively use the Past Continuous to enhance your communication.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Last night, I read a book when the lights went out.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Last night, I <strong>was reading</strong> a book when the lights went out.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The Past Continuous tense is used to show the ongoing action when the lights went out.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>While she cooked, he watched TV.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>While she <strong>was cooking</strong>, he <strong>was watching</strong> TV.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use Past Continuous to describe simultaneous actions in the past.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The <strong>Past Continuous</strong> tense describes actions that were ongoing at a specific time in the past. It is formed using <strong>was/were + verb-ing</strong>. This tense is important for setting the scene in narratives or explaining what was happening when another action occurred.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing actions that were in progress at a particular moment in the past.</li>
      <li>Setting the background scene in a story or narrative.</li>
      <li>Explaining simultaneous past actions.</li>
      <li>Indicating interrupted actions in the past.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">At 7 PM, I <strong>was reading</strong> a book.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">While they <strong>were having</strong> dinner, the phone rang.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>was/were + verb-ing</strong> to form the Past Continuous.</li>
      <li>Apply it to describe actions in progress at a specific past time.</li>
      <li>Combine with the Past Simple to show interrupted actions.</li>
      <li>Ensure the time reference is clear to avoid confusion.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using Past Simple instead of Past Continuous</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>When I arrived, they ate dinner.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>When I arrived, they <strong>were eating</strong> dinner.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use Past Continuous to show the action was ongoing at a specific past time.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Incorrectly using Past Continuous for completed actions</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Yesterday, I was finishing the report.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Yesterday, I finished the report.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use Past Simple for actions that were completed in the past.</p>
</article>
</div>
