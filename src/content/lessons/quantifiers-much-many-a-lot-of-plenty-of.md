## Real-World Examples with Quantifiers: much, many, a lot of, plenty of
### Example 1
- Weak: *There are much reasons to improve public transport.*
- Strong: *There are many reasons to improve public transport.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *The city has few money for new rail lines.*
- Strong: *The city has little money for new rail lines.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Quantifiers: much, many, a lot of, plenty of
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: mixing countable and uncountable quantifiers</summary>

- Weak: *There are much reasons to improve public transport.*
- Strong: *There are many reasons to improve public transport.*
- Fix: use many/few with countable nouns and much/little with uncountable nouns.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *The city has few money for new rail lines.*
- Strong: *The city has little money for new rail lines.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in Quantifiers: much, many, a lot of, plenty of without improving meaning</summary>

- Weak: *The writer attempts Quantifiers: much, many, a lot of, plenty of, but the sentence becomes difficult to follow due to unnecessary additions.*
- Strong: *The writer uses Quantifiers: much, many, a lot of, plenty of selectively, so the sentence remains clear and focused.*
- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
</details>
