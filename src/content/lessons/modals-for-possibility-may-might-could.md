<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Modals for possibility</strong> such as 'may', 'might', and 'could' help you express uncertainty or potential outcomes. These small words can significantly alter the meaning of a sentence, making them essential for clear communication.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She will attend the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She <strong>might</strong> attend the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Using 'might' indicates that her attendance is uncertain.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The project will be completed by next week.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The project <strong>may</strong> be completed by next week.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'May' suggests that completion by next week is possible but not guaranteed.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Modals like <strong>may</strong>, <strong>might</strong>, and <strong>could</strong> express possibility or uncertainty about an event or situation. They indicate that something is possible but not certain.</p>
    <p>Formation: <strong>subject + modal verb + base verb</strong>. These modals are used to soften statements, making them less direct and more tentative.</p>
    <p>At B2 level, understanding these nuances is crucial for expressing degrees of certainty in both spoken and written communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing uncertainty or potential outcomes in academic writing.</li>
      <li>Discussing future possibilities in business or formal contexts.</li>
      <li>Making polite suggestions or recommendations.</li>
      <li>Speculating about past events in conversation.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The project <strong>may</strong> be completed by next week.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>might</strong> attend the meeting if she finishes her report.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They <strong>could</strong> decide to postpone the event.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>may</strong>, <strong>might</strong>, and <strong>could</strong> to express possibility.</li>
      <li>Always follow the modal with the base form of the verb.</li>
      <li>Choose the modal based on the degree of certainty you wish to convey.</li>
      <li>Avoid using 'to' after these modals.</li>
      <li>Consider the context to decide which modal best fits the situation.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using 'to' after a modal verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She might to attend the conference.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She might attend the conference.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the base verb directly after the modal.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>confusing modal strength</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>They could definitely win the match.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>They might win the match.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> 'Might' is more appropriate for expressing uncertainty.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using modals for certainty instead of possibility</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She will be late to the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She may be late to the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'may' to indicate that being late is a possibility, not a certainty.</p>
</article>
</div>
