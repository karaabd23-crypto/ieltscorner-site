<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Will vs Going To</strong> helps you clarify future intentions and predictions. Choosing the correct form allows you to express whether an action is planned, spontaneous, or predicted based on evidence. This lesson will help you understand when to use each form effectively.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The future forms <strong>'will'</strong> and <strong>'going to'</strong> are used to express predictions about the future. <strong>'Will'</strong> is often used for spontaneous decisions or predictions not based on current evidence, while <strong>'going to'</strong> is used for plans and predictions based on present evidence.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, understanding these distinctions helps in making your future intentions and predictions clear in both writing and speaking.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing a spontaneous decision: "I <strong>will</strong> help you with that."</li>
      <li>Making a prediction without evidence: "It <strong>will</strong> rain tomorrow."</li>
      <li>Describing a plan or intention based on current evidence: "I am <strong>going to</strong> start a new job next week."</li>
      <li>Predicting something based on what you see: "Look at those clouds! It is <strong>going to</strong> rain."</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I <strong>will</strong> call you later.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She is <strong>going to</strong> visit her grandparents tomorrow.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They <strong>will</strong> probably win the match.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>'will'</strong> for spontaneous decisions and predictions without evidence.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>'going to'</strong> for plans and predictions based on current evidence.</li>
      <li>Both forms can express future actions, but the context determines the choice.</li>
      <li>Do not use <strong>'will'</strong> for actions based on present evidence; use <strong>'going to'</strong> instead.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using 'will' for planned actions</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> I will visit my parents this weekend.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> I am going to visit my parents this weekend.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'going to' for planned actions.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using 'going to' for spontaneous decisions</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> I am going to help you with that right now.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> I will help you with that right now.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'will' for spontaneous decisions.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Confusing predictions with plans</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> It will rain because of the dark clouds.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> It is going to rain because of the dark clouds.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'going to' for predictions based on evidence.</p>
