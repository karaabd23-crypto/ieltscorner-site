<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">CELPIP Reading has multiple question types with different difficulty levels and time costs. Allocating time by question type prevents over-spending on hard questions and under-spending on easy ones.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Vocabulary in context: 30-45s</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Find the word, read the surrounding sentence, match the definition</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Question: "What does 'adjacent' mean in paragraph 2?" Find the sentence → match to the option that fits the context.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Detail/fact question: 60-90s</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Extract keywords, scan, find evidence sentence</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"What reason does the author give for...?" Scan for the reason keyword, find the sentence, select the matching option.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Inference question: 90-120s</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">These require reading two or three sentences; budget more time</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Inference questions often contain "implies", "suggests", "can be inferred". Flag and return if over 90 seconds.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Skip-and-return rule</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">If stuck after 90 seconds, mark and move on</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Return to skipped questions in the final 2 minutes with remaining time.</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Uniform time allocation</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I give every question the same amount of time regardless of type.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I allocate 45 seconds to vocabulary questions and 90-120 seconds to inference questions.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Vocabulary questions can be answered in under a minute; inference questions genuinely need more time. Allocating uniformly wastes time on easy questions.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Not skipping stuck questions</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I stayed on the difficult question until I answered it, even when it took 3 minutes.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>After 90 seconds on an inference question with no answer, I moved on and returned with fresh eyes.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Returning to a difficult question with fresh context often resolves it faster than pressing through.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Answering from memory</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I answered from what I remembered reading, not from re-finding the evidence.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Even for questions I thought I remembered, I re-scanned for the specific sentence before selecting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Memory is unreliable under test pressure. Re-scanning takes 10-20 seconds and prevents confident wrong answers.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Reading tests speed and accuracy simultaneously. The time limit is designed so that candidates who use all their time on hard questions cannot finish the section. Type-based time allocation -- fast on vocabulary, medium on details, maximum on inference with a skip rule -- is the strategy that allows candidates to attempt every question rather than leaving the section incomplete.</p>
