<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Be</strong> is a versatile verb in English, functioning both as a main verb and as an auxiliary to form continuous and passive structures. Mastering its use is essential for clear and accurate communication.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The project is finish.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The project <strong>is</strong> finished.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use 'is' as an auxiliary verb to form the passive voice with a past participle.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She being late often.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She <strong>is</strong> often late.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use 'is' as a main verb to describe a state.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The verb <strong>Be</strong> serves a dual purpose in English: as a main verb, it describes states of being, existence, or identity. As an auxiliary verb, it helps form continuous tenses and passive voice structures. At the B2 level, mastering this dual role is crucial for expressing complex ideas clearly in both spoken and written English.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing a state or condition (e.g., "She <strong>is</strong> happy.").</li>
      <li>Forming the continuous tense (e.g., "They <strong>are</strong> running.").</li>
      <li>Constructing passive sentences (e.g., "The book <strong>was</strong> written.").</li>
      <li>Indicating existence (e.g., "There <strong>are</strong> three options.").</li>
      <li>Expressing identity (e.g., "He <strong>is</strong> a teacher.").</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>is</strong> a manager at the company.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They <strong>are</strong> being interviewed for the position.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The report <strong>was</strong> completed yesterday.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>Be</strong> to describe states or identities (e.g., "She <strong>is</strong> a student.").</li>
      <li>As an auxiliary, <strong>Be</strong> forms continuous and passive structures.</li>
      <li>Always match <strong>Be</strong> with the subject in number and tense.</li>
      <li>For passive voice, use "<strong>Be</strong> + past participle".</li>
      <li>For continuous tense, use "<strong>Be</strong> + verb-ing".</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using 'be' in the wrong form for continuous tense</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She be working on the project.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She <strong>is</strong> working on the project.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'is' with the verb-ing form for present continuous.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Incorrect passive voice construction</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The report was complete yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The report <strong>was</strong> completed yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'was' with the past participle for passive voice.</p>
</article>
</div>
