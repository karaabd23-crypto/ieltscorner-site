<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Modals of obligation and prohibition</strong> help you express rules and necessities clearly. They are crucial in formal writing and speaking to convey what is required or forbidden. In this lesson, you'll learn how to use these modals correctly to ensure your message is understood as intended.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Employees must to complete the training by Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Employees must complete the training by Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Must" is followed by the base verb without "to" to express obligation.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Visitors cannot to enter the restricted area.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Visitors cannot enter the restricted area.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Cannot" is used without "to" to indicate prohibition.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Modals for obligation and prohibition express rules and necessities. They indicate what actions are required or forbidden. Common modals for obligation include <strong>must</strong> and <strong>have to</strong>, while <strong>must not</strong> and <strong>cannot</strong> express prohibition.</p>
    <p>These modals are essential for clear communication in formal and informal settings, especially at the B2 level, where precision in expressing rules and expectations is important.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing a rule or requirement, such as in workplace policies.</li>
      <li>Stating prohibitions in formal documents or instructions.</li>
      <li>Communicating expectations in academic or professional contexts.</li>
      <li>Giving clear instructions to ensure compliance.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Employees <strong>must</strong> wear their ID badges at all times.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">You <strong>must not</strong> use your phone during the meeting.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>must</strong> or <strong>have to</strong> for strong obligations.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>must not</strong> or <strong>cannot</strong> for prohibitions.</li>
      <li>Do not add "to" after modals when using the base verb.</li>
      <li>Ensure the modal matches the level of necessity or prohibition intended.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using "to" after a modal verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>You must to attend the meeting on time.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>You must attend the meeting on time.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the base verb directly after the modal.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using the wrong modal for prohibition</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>You should not smoke in this area.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>You must not smoke in this area.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "must not" for strong prohibitions.</p>
</article>
</div>
