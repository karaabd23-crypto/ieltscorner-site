<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>CELPIP Speaking Task 7: Expressing an Opinion</strong> focuses on delivering structured and coherent opinions. This lesson guides you in crafting responses that are clear, supported by reasons and examples, and easy to follow, even under exam pressure.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span> I think this approach is good because it helps people in many ways.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span> I think this approach is beneficial because it enhances community engagement, increases accessibility, and improves overall satisfaction.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger answer provides specific benefits, making the opinion more persuasive.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span> For example, one local change made things better for some people.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span> For example, implementing a flexible work schedule in our office led to a 15% increase in employee productivity.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The better example provides specific data, making the argument more compelling.</p>
</article>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Expressing an opinion in CELPIP Speaking Task 7 involves delivering a structured response that is clear and persuasive. This task requires you to state your opinion, support it with reasons and examples, and conclude effectively. At the B2 level, mastering this skill helps you communicate your ideas logically and confidently under exam conditions.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Answering CELPIP Speaking Task 7 questions.</li>
      <li>Expressing opinions in formal or academic settings.</li>
      <li>Participating in discussions where clear argumentation is needed.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">

      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>For instance</strong>, a study showed that employees working from home completed projects 20% faster.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Start with a clear opinion statement using "I think" or "I believe".</li>
      <li>Provide one or two strong reasons to support your opinion.</li>
      <li>Include specific examples to illustrate your points.</li>
      <li>Conclude with a sentence that reinforces your opinion.</li>
      <li>Avoid using fillers; be concise and direct.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Hedging instead of stating your opinion directly</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> Well, there are many sides to this question and it is hard to say.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> In my opinion, remote work is better for employees with long commutes.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> State your opinion clearly in the first sentence using "I think" or "In my opinion".</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Giving circular reasons with no real content</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> Like, you know, it is good because it is good for people.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> Remote work reduces commute stress, which improves focus during work hours.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Replace the circular reason with a cause-and-effect chain: [change] → [specific benefit].</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Finishing without a statement that closes your opinion</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> So yes, maybe, that is my idea.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> That is why I believe this option leads to better outcomes overall.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> End with one sentence that restates your opinion in different words.</p>
</article>
</div>
