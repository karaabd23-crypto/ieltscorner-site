<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">Section 4 is an academic monologue — a lecture or talk. No conversation, no turn-taking. The speaker follows a predictable structure: introduction, main points (usually 2-3), and conclusion. Predicting this structure before the recording starts helps you know when a new answer is coming.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Weak</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Listening passively for keywords without anticipating where in the talk each answer will appear.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Stronger</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Reading the question set during the preview, inferring the lecture structure from the question types, and mentally marking "this answer comes after the speaker moves from X to Y."</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">If questions 31-35 are about "causes" and 36-40 are about "solutions," you know the lecture will pivot from causes to solutions halfway through. When the pivot language comes ("turning now to," "having established X, let us consider Y"), you know question 36 is next.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Structure prediction steps</p>
    <ol>
      <li>Read all Section 4 questions during the preview.</li>
      <li>Group questions by topic — each group usually maps to one section of the lecture.</li>
      <li>Note any signposting language cues in the questions (causes, effects, solutions, examples).</li>
      <li>Listen for the speaker's pivot phrases to know when a new question group starts.</li>
    </ol>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Common pivot phrases</p>
    <ul>
      <li>"Turning now to..." / "Moving on to..."</li>
      <li>"Having considered X, let us now look at Y."</li>
      <li>"The second factor is..." / "Finally, I want to address..."</li>
      <li>"In terms of solutions..." / "What can be done about this?"</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Prediction in practice</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Q31-33 = causes → listen for "The first/second/third cause is..."</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Q34-37 = effects → listen for "This leads to..." or "As a result..."</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Q38-40 = solutions → listen for "What can be done?" or "One approach is..."</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Questions are in the order they appear in the lecture — never skip ahead.</li>
      <li>Pivot phrases are your signal that the current question group is finished.</li>
      <li>If you miss an answer, mark a best guess and move to the next question immediately.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Not reading questions before the recording</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Using the preview time to re-read instructions instead of studying the questions.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Reading all Section 4 questions during preview to predict structure.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> You get 30 seconds of preview. That is enough to read 10 short questions and identify the structural pattern.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Losing place after missing an answer</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Missing question 33, then spending 20 seconds trying to recover it while question 34's answer passes.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Missing question 33, marking a best guess immediately, and moving focus to question 34.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> A missed answer costs 1 mark. Losing your place costs 3-4 marks. Move on instantly.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">Section 4 is consistently the lowest-scoring section for IELTS candidates because it is the fastest and most complex. Structure prediction converts an unpredictable monologue into a navigable sequence. Candidates who predict structure and recognise pivot phrases maintain focus across all 10 questions; those who listen passively typically lose 3-4 marks when the lecture changes topic.</p>
