<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Present Perfect Continuous</strong> helps you express actions that began in the past and continue to the present, emphasizing the duration or ongoing nature. This lesson will guide you in using this tense effectively to convey clear timelines and ongoing actions.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>I am living here since 2020.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I <strong>have been living</strong> here since 2020.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use the Present Perfect Continuous to show an action that started in the past and continues to the present.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She works on the report for two hours.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She <strong>has been working</strong> on the report for two hours.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The Present Perfect Continuous emphasizes the ongoing nature of her work.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The <strong>Present Perfect Continuous</strong> tense is used to describe actions that started in the past and are still continuing or have recently stopped, with an emphasis on the duration or ongoing nature of the action. It is formed using <strong>has/have been + present participle</strong> (verb + -ing). This tense is important at the B2 level for expressing ongoing actions and their relevance to the present.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing actions that began in the past and are still continuing.</li>
      <li>Emphasizing the duration of an activity up to the present moment.</li>
      <li>Discussing actions that have recently stopped but have present relevance.</li>
      <li>In contexts where the action's duration is important, such as work projects or studies.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>has been studying</strong> for three hours.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They <strong>have been working</strong> on the project since last month.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>has/have been + verb-ing</strong> to form the Present Perfect Continuous.</li>
      <li>Apply this tense to actions that are ongoing or have a connection to the present.</li>
      <li>Do not use with specific past time markers like 'yesterday' or 'last year.'</li>
      <li>Emphasize the action's duration or ongoing nature.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using simple present for ongoing actions</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>I work here since 2015.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>I <strong>have been working</strong> here since 2015.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use Present Perfect Continuous for actions that started in the past and continue to the present.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Ignoring the ongoing nature of actions</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She writes the report since morning.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She <strong>has been writing</strong> the report since morning.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the Present Perfect Continuous to highlight the duration of ongoing actions.</p>
</article>
</div>
