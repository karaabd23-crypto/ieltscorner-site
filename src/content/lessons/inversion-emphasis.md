<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Inversion for Emphasis</strong> allows you to highlight key information in a sentence by altering the usual word order. This technique is particularly useful in formal writing and speeches where you want to draw attention to specific details without altering the overall meaning.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Never I have seen such a confusing timetable.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Never have I seen such a confusing timetable.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Never' at the start of the sentence requires inversion, placing the auxiliary verb before the subject.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Only after the final practice she felt ready.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Only after the final practice did she feel ready.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Only after' triggers inversion, necessitating the use of 'did' before the subject.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Inversion for emphasis involves reversing the typical subject-verb order to highlight a particular part of the sentence. It is often used with negative adverbials or expressions to create a more dramatic effect. This structure is important at the B2 level as it enhances the sophistication and variety of your writing.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Beginning a sentence with a negative adverbial phrase (e.g., 'Never', 'Rarely').</li>
      <li>Emphasizing an unexpected or surprising event.</li>
      <li>Writing formal texts or speeches where emphasis is required.</li>
      <li>Creating a dramatic effect in storytelling or persuasive writing.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Never</strong> have I seen such a confusing timetable.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Only after</strong> the final practice <strong>did</strong> she feel ready.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use inversion with negative adverbials at the start of a sentence.</li>
      <li>Ensure auxiliary verbs are correctly placed after the adverbial.</li>
      <li>Check that the sentence remains clear and grammatically correct.</li>
      <li>Remember that inversion adds emphasis and formality.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>changing word order for emphasis without keeping the grammar accurate</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Rarely we find such clear instructions in practice books.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Rarely do we find such clear instructions in practice books.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Inversion requires the auxiliary 'do' to precede the subject after 'Rarely'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>forgetting inversion after a negative fronted phrase</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Rarely we see such a clear chart in class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Rarely do we see such a clear chart in class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Negative adverbials like 'Rarely' require inversion with 'do'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>breaking agreement inside a cleft-like structure</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>What students need most are clearer homework instructions.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>What students need most is clearer homework instruction.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Ensure subject-verb agreement when using cleft structures.</p>
</article>
</div>
