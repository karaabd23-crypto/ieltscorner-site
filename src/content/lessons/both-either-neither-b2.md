## Topic Explanation and Use
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Core idea</p>
    <p>These paired forms only work well when both sides match.</p>
<p>Correlative pairs such as both ... and, either ... or, and neither ... nor connect two matching choices. The structure works best when both parts of the pair are parallel.</p>
<p>Use these pairs when you want to add, limit, or balance options clearly. The reader should be able to predict the second half once the first half begins.</p>
<p>At B2 level, build one correct base sentence first, then add detail without breaking grammar control.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it here</p>
    <ul><li>Keep the grammar parallel on both sides of the pair.</li><li>Make sure the verb agrees with the real subject in either/or and neither/nor patterns.</li><li>Use the pair only when you are linking two similar sentence parts.</li></ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Watch it work</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack"><div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Both the teacher and the students were ready on time.</div>
<div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Either the library or the classrooms are open this evening.</div></div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Remember this</p>
    <ul><li>Choose the correct pair: both ... and, either ... or, or neither ... nor.</li><li>Keep the grammar parallel on both sides of the pair.</li><li>Check the verb agreement after either/or or neither/nor.</li><li>Make sure the pair links two similar sentence parts.</li><li>Read the whole sentence to confirm that the balance feels natural.</li></ul>
  </section>

## Common Errors with Both, Either, Neither
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>breaking parallel structure or agreement in a correlative pair</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Both the teacher and the students was ready on time.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Both the teacher and the students were ready on time.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> keep the pair parallel and match the verb to the real subject near it when needed</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>breaking the pair with unmatched grammar</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Both to revise grammar and vocabulary practice are useful.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Both revising grammar and practising vocabulary are useful.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> keep both sides of the pair in the same grammar shape</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using a singular verb with a plural nearer subject</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Neither the principal nor the teachers was happy with the change.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Neither the principal nor the teachers were happy with the change.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> check the nearer subject in neither/nor patterns</p>
</article>
</div>
