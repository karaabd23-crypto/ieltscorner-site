<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>IELTS Writing Task 2: Problem-Solution</strong> requires you to clearly define a problem and propose solutions that effectively address it. A strong essay not only describes the problem but also provides practical and well-explained solutions. This lesson will guide you on how to maintain a clear connection between the problem and the solution throughout your essay.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>There are many issues with pollution in cities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Urban pollution is primarily caused by vehicle emissions, which can be mitigated by promoting electric public transport.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger sentence identifies a specific cause and proposes a targeted solution.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>We should improve education systems.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Implementing technology-based learning can address the lack of resources in rural schools, improving educational outcomes.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The better version specifies a solution and links it to the identified problem.</p>
</article>
</div>


## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>In an IELTS Writing Task 2: Problem-Solution essay, you are required to identify a specific problem and propose one or more solutions. This type of essay assesses your ability to clearly explain the problem, analyze its causes, and suggest practical solutions that are logically connected to the problem. Mastery of this structure is crucial at the B2 level to achieve coherence and task achievement.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Writing an essay that requires you to discuss a specific problem and propose solutions.</li>
      <li>Analyzing the causes of a problem to provide context for your solutions.</li>
      <li>Developing solutions that are directly linked to the identified problem.</li>
      <li>Ensuring each paragraph has a clear role, either explaining the problem or detailing a solution.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Problem:</strong> Traffic congestion in urban areas is increasing due to inadequate public transport.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Solution:</strong> Developing efficient public transport systems can reduce reliance on private cars and alleviate congestion.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Clearly define the problem in the introduction.</li>
      <li>Analyze the causes of the problem before proposing solutions.</li>
      <li>Ensure each solution directly addresses the problem and its causes.</li>
      <li>Use logical transitions to connect the problem and solutions.</li>
      <li>Conclude by summarizing the problem and reiterating the effectiveness of the solutions.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>describing the problem without identifying its cause</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Traffic congestion is a big problem in many cities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Traffic congestion worsens in cities where public transport infrastructure has not kept pace with population growth.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Add a cause clause (because, due to, as a result of) after naming the problem.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>proposing a solution without explaining how it solves the problem</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The government should invest more money. This will fix the problem.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Investing in metro expansion would reduce private car use, which is the primary driver of congestion.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Link your solution to the cause: [solution] → [how it addresses the cause] → [result].</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>mixing causes and solutions in the same paragraph</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Traffic is caused by poor infrastructure. Also the government should build more roads. People cause traffic too.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The main cause of congestion is reliance on private cars. To address this, governments could invest in affordable public transport.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Separate causes and solutions: one paragraph for causes, one for solutions.</p>
</article>
</div>
