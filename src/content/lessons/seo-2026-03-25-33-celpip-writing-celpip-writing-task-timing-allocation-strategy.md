<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">CELPIP Writing gives you 26 minutes total: 8 for Task 1 (email) and 18 for Task 2 (survey response). Candidates who run out of time on Task 2 or rush Task 1 lose marks on Content and Coherence, not just length.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Task 1: 2-1-4-1</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">2 min read, 1 min plan, 4 min write, 1 min check</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Read prompt + underline bullets (2) → note opening + body + close (1) → draft (4) → fix errors (1)</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Task 2: 3-2-11-2</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">3 min read, 2 min outline, 11 min write, 2 min review</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Read and categorise questions (3) → bullet your answers (2) → draft all responses (11) → check and correct (2)</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Clock anchor</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Note start time and set a mental midpoint</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Starting Task 2 at 8:00 → midpoint check at 8:09 to confirm half the response is drafted</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Minimum viable answer</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">If running behind, shorten elaboration, not structure</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Write all required components at a lower word count rather than omitting a section entirely</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Over-elaborating Task 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Spending 12 of 26 minutes on Task 1 email</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Finishing Task 1 in 7-8 minutes</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Task 1 is shorter (150 words). A longer response does not score higher if it just repeats the same points.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Skipping the plan</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Starting to write Task 2 immediately without an outline</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Spending 2 minutes bulleting your answers before writing</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> A 2-minute outline prevents mid-response confusion and saves more time than it costs.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">No review buffer</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Writing until the timer runs out with no check pass</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Saving 1-2 minutes to check articles, tense, and subject-verb agreement</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> A final read catches surface errors that cost accuracy marks.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Writing is time-pressured by design. Candidates who run out of time on Task 2 produce incomplete responses that score CLB 7 on Content regardless of language quality. A structured time plan of 8 and 18 minutes, each divided into read, plan, write, and check phases, is the single most reliable way to protect your Task Fulfilment score under exam conditions.</p>
