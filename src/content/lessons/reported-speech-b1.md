## Goal
Learn how to use **Direct and Indirect Speech** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What Direct and Indirect Speech Means
This lesson is specifically about **Direct and Indirect Speech**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
Direct and Indirect Speech controls how a sentence carries meaning: reference, sequence, and emphasis must stay clear from start to finish.

Use Direct and Indirect Speech to make one precise meaning per sentence, then expand only when the added structure improves clarity for exam readers.

Use conditions:
- Choose the target form after deciding the exact meaning (fact, contrast, cause, or condition).
- Keep agreement and word order stable before adding extra clauses or modifiers.
- If a longer sentence becomes harder to scan, split it and keep the same logic.

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate Direct and Indirect Speech helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When Direct and Indirect Speech is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Choose the exact grammar job first: where **Direct and Indirect Speech** controls time, relationship, or emphasis.
2. Build a complete base clause, then place **Direct and Indirect Speech** in the position required by the pattern.
3. Check agreement and tense around **Direct and Indirect Speech** before adding extra words.
4. Add one supporting clause only if the logic stays clear in public policy and daily life context.
5. Re-read for one specific risk: wrong form, wrong position, or mixed timeline.

- Strong: *The sentence uses Direct and Indirect Speech and the forms are matched to the intended meaning.*

- Fix: prefer simpler structure when added complexity does not improve precision.
</details>
