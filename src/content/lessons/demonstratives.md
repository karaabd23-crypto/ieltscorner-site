<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Using 'That' as a Demonstrative Pronoun</strong> helps you point to specific people or things without repeating the same noun. This lesson will teach you how to use 'that' to maintain clarity and avoid redundancy in your sentences, especially when referring to items not immediately present.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>I like this movie, but the other is better.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I like this movie, but <strong>that</strong> one is better.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Using 'that' makes it clear which movie is being referred to without repeating 'movie'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Today's weather is pleasant, but yesterday's was nicer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Today's weather is pleasant, but <strong>that</strong> of yesterday was nicer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'That' replaces 'yesterday's weather' to avoid repetition.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>'That' as a demonstrative pronoun is used to refer to specific people, objects, or ideas that are not near the speaker. It replaces a noun to avoid repetition and enhance clarity. At the B2 level, mastering this usage helps in making communication more precise and concise.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Referring back to something mentioned earlier in a conversation or text.</li>
      <li>Pointing out something specific that is not close to the speaker.</li>
      <li>Contrasting two items, where 'that' refers to the less immediate one.</li>
      <li>In formal writing to maintain clarity and avoid redundancy.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I prefer this book, but <strong>that</strong> one is also interesting.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The weather today is nice, but <strong>that</strong> of yesterday was better.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'that' to refer to something not immediately present.</li>
      <li>Ensure the noun 'that' replaces is clear from context.</li>
      <li>Use 'that' for contrast when comparing two items.</li>
      <li>Avoid using 'that' if it creates ambiguity; repeat the noun if necessary.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using 'that' without a clear reference</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She mentioned the report, but that was unclear.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She mentioned the report, but <strong>that</strong> report was unclear.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Ensure 'that' clearly refers to 'the report'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Confusing 'that' with 'this'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This book is interesting, but this one is better.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>This book is interesting, but <strong>that</strong> one is better.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'that' to refer to the book not immediately present.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance"><strong>&#127919; Why it matters:</strong> Using 'that' as a demonstrative pronoun enhances your writing by making it more concise and clear. It allows you to avoid repetition and ensures that your reader understands exactly what you are referring to, which is crucial for effective communication, especially in formal and academic contexts.</p>
