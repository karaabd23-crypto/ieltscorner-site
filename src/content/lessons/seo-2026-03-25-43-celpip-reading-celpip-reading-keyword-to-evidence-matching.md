<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">Most CELPIP Reading errors happen because candidates match the topic of a question to a passage section but do not confirm the specific evidence. This lesson teaches exact keyword-to-evidence matching to eliminate guessing.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Keyword extraction</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Underline the 2-3 key content words in the question</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Question: "What caused the delay in construction?" Keywords: caused, delay, construction</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Scan not read</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Move your eye quickly until the keyword or its synonym appears</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Scan for "delay", "construction", "postpone", "halt" -- any synonym of the keyword</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Evidence sentence</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Find the specific sentence that contains the answer; do not paraphrase the paragraph</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"Due to material shortages, the project was postponed by six months." This sentence directly answers the question.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Distractor check</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Before selecting, verify the other options are not supported by this sentence</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">The sentence mentions "shortages" but not "budget" or "weather" -- reject those options.</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Topic matching not evidence matching</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>The paragraph is about pricing so I picked the pricing answer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The paragraph mentions "raw material costs" as the cause; the answer must use that phrasing or a direct synonym.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> CELPIP distractors are usually on the same topic as the correct answer. Evidence matching, not topic matching, distinguishes them.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Reading instead of scanning</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I read the whole passage twice and ran out of time.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I extracted three keywords from the question and scanned for any synonym in the passage.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Scanning moves three to four times faster than reading. CELPIP Reading is timed -- scanning is required, not optional.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Synonym blindness</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>The question uses "postpone" but I only looked for "postpone" in the passage.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The passage uses "delayed" -- a synonym -- which answers the question about being postponed.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Prepare a mental synonym set for common exam topics: delayed/postponed/halted; cost/price/fee; require/need/necessitate.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Reading is designed to test whether you can locate specific information quickly, not whether you can understand a passage after reading it three times. The keyword-to-evidence matching method converts a comprehension task into a targeted search task, which is both faster and more accurate. Candidates who use this approach consistently reduce their error rate on information-location questions by identifying the exact evidence sentence rather than inferring from the paragraph topic.</p>
