<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Cleft Sentences</strong> allow you to emphasize specific parts of a sentence by restructuring it into two clauses. This technique helps you highlight important information, making your communication more effective and nuanced. In this lesson, you'll learn how to use cleft sentences to add emphasis while maintaining clarity.</p></div>

## Examples

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Cleft sentences are a grammatical structure that divides a sentence into two clauses to emphasize a specific part. This structure highlights the most important information, making it stand out to the reader or listener. A typical cleft sentence pattern involves using 'It is/was' or 'What' followed by the emphasized element and a relative clause. At the B2 level, mastering cleft sentences enhances clarity and emphasis in communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>You need to emphasize a specific part of a sentence in formal writing or speech.</li>
      <li>You want to highlight key information in academic papers or reports.</li>
      <li>You are clarifying which part of a sentence is most critical in complex explanations.</li>
      <li>You aim to create a dramatic effect in storytelling or presentations.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>It was</strong> John <strong>who</strong> completed the project on time.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>What</strong> she needs <strong>is</strong> more time to finish the task.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'It is/was' to emphasize a noun or noun phrase.</li>
      <li>Use 'What' to emphasize an action or idea.</li>
      <li>Ensure the cleft sentence retains the original meaning.</li>
      <li>Check that the sentence remains grammatically correct after restructuring.</li>
      <li>Avoid overusing cleft sentences to maintain a natural flow.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Misplacing the emphasized element in a cleft sentence</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> It is the budget approved by the manager.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> It was the manager who approved the budget.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Ensure the cleft structure correctly emphasizes the intended element.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using cleft sentences unnecessarily</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> It was a sunny day that we went to the beach.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> We went to the beach on a sunny day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use cleft sentences only when emphasis is needed.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Incorrect verb agreement in cleft sentences</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span> What they need are more time to prepare.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span> What they need is more time to prepare.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Ensure subject-verb agreement in cleft sentences.</p>
</article>
</div>
