## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Symbols for common words</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Replace frequent words with symbols to write faster</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"because" = bc; "therefore" = .". ; "increase" = up arrow; "decrease" = down arrow; "approximately" = ~</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Subject-verb-object only</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Capture who, what, and when; skip adjectives and filler</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"The new community centre will open next spring" → "CC open spring"</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Number and name priority</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Always capture numbers, names, dates, and locations verbatim</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">If the speaker says "$4.2 million by March 2026", write "$4.2M Mar26" -- exact values are answer anchors</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">New topic = new line</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Start a new line when the speaker shifts topic to keep notes scannable</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">If the speaker moves from budget to staffing, start a new line: "staff: 3 new hires -- contracts -- Q3"</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Writing sentences</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>The manager told the team that the budget would be cut by 15 percent.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>mgr → team: budget -15%</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Full sentences while listening cause you to fall behind by two or three sentences. Symbols and abbreviations keep you current.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Capturing adjectives not numbers</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>"A significant budget reduction was announced for the upcoming quarter."</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>"budget -15% Q3"</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Adjectives like "significant" are answered by the number. Write the number, not the adjective.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Not separating topics</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>All notes on one line, no topic breaks</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Budget line / Staffing line / Timeline line</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Scannable notes with topic separations let you find the answer to each question without re-reading everything.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Listening audio plays once. The note-taking challenge is not comprehension -- it is capture speed. Candidates who write full sentences consistently fall two or three sentences behind the speaker and miss key details. A compact note system (symbols, SVO structure, number priority, topic line breaks) keeps you current with the audio while creating a scannable reference for answering questions immediately after the recording ends.</p>
