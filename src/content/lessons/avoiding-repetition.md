<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Avoiding Repetition in Writing</strong> is a crucial skill for achieving clarity and coherence in IELTS and CELPIP exams. This lesson demonstrates how to enhance writing by using varied language and avoiding redundancy, ensuring each sentence contributes effectively to the overall message.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>Education is important. Education helps people. Education creates opportunities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Education is important because it helps people and creates new opportunities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger sentence combines repeated ideas into one coherent statement.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>The project was successful. The project met its goals.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The project was successful in meeting its goals.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The improved sentence avoids redundancy by combining ideas.</p>
</article>
</div>


## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Avoiding repetition in writing means using diverse language and synonyms to convey ideas without redundancy. This enhances clarity and coherence, ensuring each sentence contributes uniquely to the text. At the B2 level, mastering this skill can significantly boost writing scores in exams like IELTS and CELPIP by making arguments clearer and more engaging.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Writing essays or reports where clarity and variety are crucial.</li>
      <li>Responding to exam prompts requiring detailed explanations.</li>
      <li>Editing drafts to improve readability and engagement.</li>
      <li>Crafting complex sentences to express nuanced ideas.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Instead of saying "<strong>Education is important. Education helps people. Education creates opportunities.</strong>", say "<strong>Education is important because it helps people and creates new opportunities.</strong>"</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Instead of "<strong>The project was successful. The project met its goals.</strong>", say "<strong>The project was successful in meeting its goals.</strong>"</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use synonyms to avoid repeating the same word.</li>
      <li>Combine sentences to eliminate redundancy.</li>
      <li>Replace nouns with pronouns when the reference is clear.</li>
      <li>Paraphrase ideas instead of echoing them.</li>
      <li>Ensure each sentence adds new information or perspective.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>repeating the exact same noun in every sentence</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Education is important. Education helps people. Education creates opportunities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Education is important because it helps people and creates new opportunities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Combine repeated ideas into one sentence or replace the noun with a pronoun or synonym.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using a pronoun whose reference is ambiguous</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Teachers help students because they know the subject.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Teachers help students because teachers have specialised subject knowledge.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> If two nouns could match the pronoun, replace it with the exact noun you mean.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>echoing the task question word-for-word instead of paraphrasing</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Some people think that technology is good. Do you agree that technology is good?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Some people argue that technology benefits society. I largely agree with this view.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Paraphrase the question using synonyms and a different sentence structure.</p>
</article>
</div>
