## Goal
Learn how to use **Passives: Formation and Use** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What Passives: Formation and Use Means
This lesson is specifically about **Passives: Formation and Use**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
Passives: Formation and Use controls sentence focus. Passive forms move attention from the doer to the action/result when that focus is more useful.

Use Passives: Formation and Use when outcome matters more than agent, or when agent is unknown, obvious, or unnecessary in exam writing.

Use conditions:
- Form passive with be + past participle and keep tense accurate.
- Include the agent only when it adds important information.
- Prefer active voice when it makes responsibility clearer.

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate Passives helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When Passives is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Choose the exact grammar job first: where **Passives** controls time, relationship, or emphasis.
2. Build a complete base clause, then place **Passives** in the position required by the pattern.
3. Check agreement and tense around **Passives** before adding extra words.
4. Add one supporting clause only if the logic stays clear in public policy and daily life context.
5. Re-read for one specific risk: wrong form, wrong position, or mixed timeline.

## Real-World Examples with Passives: Formation and Use
### Example 1
- Weak: *People recycle more plastic in this city every year.*
- Strong: *More plastic is recycled in this city every year.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *The committee will announce the results tomorrow.*
- Strong: *The results will be announced tomorrow.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Passives: Formation and Use
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: using an active verb form where passive focus is required</summary>

- Weak: *People recycle more plastic in this city every year.*
- Strong: *More plastic is recycled in this city every year.*
- Fix: use be + past participle and keep tense accurate.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *The committee will announce the results tomorrow.*
- Strong: *The results will be announced tomorrow.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in Passives: Formation and Use without improving meaning</summary>

- Weak: *The writer attempts Passives: Formation and Use, but the sentence becomes difficult to follow due to unnecessary additions.*
- Strong: *The writer uses Passives: Formation and Use selectively, so the sentence remains clear and focused.*
- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
</details>
