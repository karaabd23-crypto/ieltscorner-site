## Goal
Learn how to use **Must and have to** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What Must and have to Means
This lesson is specifically about **Must and have to**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
Must and have to expresses obligation strength and source: internal necessity, rules, and external requirements.

Use Must and have to to show whether the obligation comes from personal authority (must) or external systems and rules (have to).

Use conditions:
- Use must for strong speaker-imposed obligation in formal guidance.
- Use have to for external requirements, schedules, and institutional rules.
- Use do/does/did with have to in negatives and questions (do not have to, did you have to).

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate Must and have to helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When Must and have to is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Use must + base verb for strong obligation without to.
2. Use have to for external rules, schedules, or institutional requirements.
3. Use do/does/did with have to in negatives and questions.
4. Use must not for prohibition and do not have to for lack of necessity.
5. Match obligation form to meaning before adding extra detail.

## Real-World Examples with Must and have to
### Example 1
- Weak: *Students must to submit the assignment by Friday.*
- Strong: *Students must submit the assignment by Friday.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *I must to wake up early for work.*
- Strong: *I have to wake up early for work.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Must and have to
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: adding to after must</summary>

- Weak: *Students must to submit the assignment by Friday.*
- Strong: *Students must submit the assignment by Friday.*
- Fix: use must + base verb without to.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using must where external requirement is clearer with have to</summary>

- Weak: *Employees must arrive at 9 because the contract says so.*
- Strong: *Employees have to arrive at 9 because the contract says so.*
- Fix: use have to when obligation comes from external rules or contracts.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: confusing prohibition and lack of necessity</summary>

- Weak: *You must not bring your ID; it is optional.*
- Strong: *You do not have to bring your ID; it is optional.*
- Fix: must not means prohibition, while do not have to means not necessary.
</details>
