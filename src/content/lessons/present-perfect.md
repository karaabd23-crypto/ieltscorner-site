<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Present Perfect Simple</strong> helps you express actions that are relevant to the present moment. This tense is crucial for indicating experiences, changes, and ongoing situations without specifying exact times. In this lesson, you will learn how to match form to meaning effectively.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>I lived here since 2020.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I have lived here since 2020.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use 'have lived' to indicate an action that started in the past and continues to the present.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She has finished her task yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She finished her task yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Finished' is correct because 'yesterday' is a specific past time.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The <strong>Present Perfect Simple</strong> tense is used to express actions that occurred at an unspecified time before now. It highlights the relevance of past actions to the present moment.</p>
    <p>Formation: <strong>have/has + past participle</strong>.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering this tense helps in discussing experiences, changes, and ongoing situations without specifying exact times.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Talking about life experiences without mentioning when they happened.</li>
      <li>Describing changes over time.</li>
      <li>Indicating actions that started in the past and continue to the present.</li>
      <li>Discussing recent events with relevance to the present.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>I have visited</strong> Paris three times.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>She has worked</strong> here since 2015.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>have/has + past participle</strong> for the Present Perfect Simple.</li>
      <li>Avoid specific time markers like "yesterday" or "last year" with this tense.</li>
      <li>Use it to discuss life experiences or actions affecting the present.</li>
      <li>Remember: "for" and "since" often accompany this tense.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>mixing present perfect with finished time markers like yesterday</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We have seen that movie last week.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We saw that movie last week.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> use present perfect for unfinished time or life experience</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using present perfect with a finished time marker</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She has finished the report yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She finished the report yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> use a past form with finished time markers like yesterday or last week</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>switching tense without a time reason</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The study started in May and shows strong results in June.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The study started in May and showed strong results in June.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> keep the tense stable unless the timeline genuinely changes</p>
</article>
</div>
