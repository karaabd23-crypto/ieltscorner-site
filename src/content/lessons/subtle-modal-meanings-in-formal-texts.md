<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Using Modal Verbs for Formal Writing Precision</strong> enhances your ability to convey nuanced meaning in formal writing. Mastering modal verbs allows you to express degrees of certainty, obligation, and possibility with clarity and appropriateness, making your writing sound more professional and credible.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>We will finish the project soon.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>We <strong>should</strong> finish the project by the end of the month.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Using "should" adds a level of expectation and formality.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>This plan is good and might work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>This plan <strong>might</strong> prove effective if implemented correctly.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Might" conveys possibility in a formal context.</p>
</article>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Modal verbs are auxiliary verbs used to express possibility, necessity, or permission. In formal writing, they help convey degrees of certainty and obligation with precision.</p>
    <p>Common modal verbs include <strong>can</strong>, <strong>could</strong>, <strong>may</strong>, <strong>might</strong>, <strong>must</strong>, <strong>shall</strong>, <strong>should</strong>, <strong>will</strong>, and <strong>would</strong>.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, using modal verbs accurately can enhance the clarity and professionalism of your writing.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing possibility or probability in a formal context (e.g., "This policy <strong>may</strong> improve efficiency.").</li>
      <li>Indicating necessity or obligation (e.g., "Participants <strong>must</strong> adhere to the guidelines.").</li>
      <li>Making formal suggestions or recommendations (e.g., "The committee <strong>should</strong> consider alternative options.").</li>
      <li>Discussing hypothetical situations (e.g., "If implemented, the plan <strong>would</strong> reduce costs.").</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The new policy <strong>might</strong> lead to increased productivity.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Employees <strong>must</strong> submit reports by the end of the week.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">It <strong>could</strong> be beneficial to reassess the current strategy.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>may</strong> and <strong>might</strong> to express possibility or uncertainty.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>must</strong> to express necessity or obligation.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>should</strong> for recommendations or advice.</li>
      <li>Avoid using casual modal phrases like "gonna" or "wanna" in formal writing.</li>
      <li>Ensure the modal verb aligns with the intended level of certainty or obligation.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using casual language instead of modal verbs for formal precision</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We are gonna finish the project soon.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We <strong>should</strong> complete the project by the end of the month.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Replace casual phrases with formal modal verbs to express obligation or expectation.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using absolute statements without modal verbs</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This solution will definitely work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>This solution <strong>might</strong> work under certain conditions.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use modal verbs to indicate possibility rather than certainty.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>ignoring modal verbs for recommendations</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>You have to consider other options.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>You <strong>should</strong> consider other options.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "should" to make recommendations in a formal context.</p>
</article>
</div>
