<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">CELPIP Speaking Task 2 asks you to tell a story based on pictures. The key is controlling your narrative so it has a clear beginning, middle, and end within 60 seconds -- without running out of things to say or losing the thread.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Weak</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>"So there is a person and they are doing something and then something happens and it is a problem and then at the end it gets better."</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Stronger</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>"In the first image, a young woman is rushing out of her apartment with her work bag, looking anxious. By the second image, she has missed the bus and is standing alone at the stop. In the final scene, a colleague is pulling over to offer her a ride, and she looks relieved."</p>
  
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Image-by-image structure</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Narrate one image per sentence block</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"In the first picture... In the second... In the final image..."</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Character and emotion</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Name the person and their emotional state</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"A middle-aged man looks frustrated as he waits in a long queue."</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Cause and consequence</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Link images with cause and consequence language</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"Because the alarm did not go off, she arrived 20 minutes late to the interview."</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Story arc</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Tension in the middle image; resolution in the final image</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"The problem is resolved when the manager agrees to reschedule."</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Describing not narrating</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>There is a table. There is a chair. There is a window.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>A man sits alone at a table, staring out the window, clearly waiting for someone who has not arrived.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Combine objects into a scene with a character whose situation gives the story meaning.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Losing the thread</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>So in the first picture... and then... I am not sure what happens next...</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>By the second image, the conflict has escalated and the outcome is unclear.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Prepare a fallback phrase for uncertain images: "By this point in the story..." anchors you without claiming certainty.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">No resolution</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>And that is the end of the story.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The story ends with the two colleagues shaking hands, suggesting the disagreement has been resolved.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Interpret the final image as a resolution even if it is ambiguous. Examiners reward narrative closure.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Speaking Task 2 is a narrative task, not a description task. Candidates who list objects score below CLB 8 on Coherence and Cohesion because there is no discernible story structure. A three-part arc -- scene, conflict, resolution -- fulfils the task requirement and gives the examiner the cohesive structure they are looking for even when the image sequence is ambiguous.</p>
