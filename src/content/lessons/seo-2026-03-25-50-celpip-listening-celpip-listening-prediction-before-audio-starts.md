<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">CELPIP Listening gives you a few seconds to read the question before the audio plays. Candidates who use this preview time to predict the answer type -- number, name, reason, or action -- find answers faster and miss fewer details.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Answer type prediction</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Decide before the audio what kind of answer you need: number, name, date, reason, or action</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"What did the speaker recommend?" → predict: action verb + object ("register early", "contact the office")</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Keyword anchors</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Pick 2-3 words from the question to anchor your listening scan</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"Why did the company relocate?" → anchors: company, relocate, reason/because</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Audio entry point prediction</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Decide which part of the conversation likely contains the answer</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"At the end of the announcement, the speaker typically states the action step." -- position yourself to listen for it.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Distractor alertness</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Once you hear your keyword, listen one sentence further before committing</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">The speaker may say "many thought the reason was cost -- but actually it was location." Listening one sentence further captures the real answer.</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Reading but not predicting</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I read the question but just waited for the audio to start.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I read the question, predicted the answer type (date), and prepared my keyword anchors (opened, launched, began).</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Prediction converts passive reading into active preparation. The extra 10 seconds of thinking before the audio replaces 20 seconds of searching during it.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Listening for everything</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I tried to understand everything the speaker said equally.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I listened actively for my keyword anchors and captured surrounding detail when I found them.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Focused listening on keyword anchors is faster and more accurate than broad comprehension listening under time pressure.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Stopping at the first answer candidate</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I heard a number and immediately chose it as the answer without confirming.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I heard a number, listened one sentence further to confirm it was not a contrast or correction, then captured it.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Speakers often present incorrect options before giving the correct one ("not X -- actually Y"). Listening one sentence further catches corrections.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Listening preview time is a built-in advantage that unprepared candidates waste. The preview strategy -- predict answer type, set keyword anchors, listen actively -- converts the listening task from a comprehension exercise into a targeted search. Candidates who use preview time deliberately find answers more quickly, miss fewer details, and spend less cognitive effort during the recording because their attention is directed rather than broad.</p>
