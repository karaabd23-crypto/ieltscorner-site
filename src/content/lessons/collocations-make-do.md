<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Collocations: Make and Do</strong> focuses on mastering specific word pairings that enhance fluency and naturalness in English. Learn how to use "make" and "do" in the right contexts to communicate effectively.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>Students should do progress every week.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Students should make progress every week.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Make progress" is the correct collocation, enhancing clarity and naturalness.</p>

  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The team made a decision after the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Made a decision" is the proper collocation, which sounds more natural.</p>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Collocations with "make" and "do" are specific word pairings commonly used in English to convey precise meanings and sound natural. For example, we say "make a decision" instead of "do a decision." Mastery of these collocations is crucial at the B2 level to enhance fluency and accuracy.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing actions or tasks, such as "make a plan" or "do homework."</li>
      <li>Writing formal documents where precise language is required, like reports or proposals.</li>
      <li>Engaging in spoken responses during exams or interviews to demonstrate fluency.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">You need to <strong>make a decision</strong> before the deadline.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I always <strong>do my homework</strong> after dinner.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use "make" for creating or producing something, like "make a cake."</li>
      <li>Use "do" for activities or tasks, such as "do the dishes."</li>
      <li>"Make" often implies a result, while "do" focuses on the process.</li>
      <li>Learn common collocations as fixed expressions to avoid errors.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using the wrong verb with a noun</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We did research and then did a conclusion.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We did research and then made a conclusion.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Learn the correct verb-noun combinations to avoid errors.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Confusing similar verbs</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She took progress after practice.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She made progress after practice.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "make" with "progress" to maintain natural usage.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Not using collocations in sentences</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Take responsibility.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Students should take responsibility for checking their final answer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Always practice collocations in full sentences to reinforce learning.</p>
</article>
</div>
