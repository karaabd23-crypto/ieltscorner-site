<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Must, Need To, Have To</strong> are essential for expressing different levels of obligation and necessity in English. Understanding their nuances helps in conveying the right degree of urgency or requirement in various contexts, such as academic, professional, and daily communication.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>You must to submit the form before Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>You must submit the form before Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Must' is followed directly by the base verb without 'to'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>He need to go to the doctor.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>He needs to go to the doctor.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Need to' requires agreement with the subject, hence 'needs'.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p><strong>Must</strong>, <strong>Need To</strong>, and <strong>Have To</strong> are modal verbs used to express obligation and necessity. They indicate how necessary an action is. 'Must' often implies a strong obligation or necessity, 'need to' suggests requirement, and 'have to' indicates an external obligation or requirement.</p>
    <p>Formation: <strong>Subject + must/need to/have to + base verb</strong>.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, understanding these nuances is crucial for effective communication in both formal and informal settings.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing a strong obligation or necessity (e.g., "You <strong>must</strong> finish the report by tomorrow.").</li>
      <li>Indicating a requirement or necessity (e.g., "You <strong>need to</strong> submit the application by Friday.").</li>
      <li>Describing an external obligation (e.g., "Employees <strong>have to</strong> wear uniforms.").</li>
      <li>In formal writing or professional contexts to convey rules or laws.</li>
      <li>In spoken English to give advice or instructions.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">You <strong>must</strong> wear a seatbelt at all times.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">We <strong>need to</strong> complete the project by next week.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They <strong>have to</strong> submit their taxes by April 15th.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>must</strong> for strong, internal obligations or rules.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>need to</strong> when expressing necessity or requirement.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>have to</strong> for external obligations or requirements.</li>
      <li>Never add 'to' after 'must'.</li>
      <li>Choose the modal based on the strength of obligation you wish to convey.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using 'to' after 'must'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>You must to submit the form before Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>You must submit the form before Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> 'Must' is followed directly by the base verb.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Incorrect subject-verb agreement with 'need to'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He need to go to the doctor.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He needs to go to the doctor.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Ensure 'need' agrees with the subject (e.g., 'he needs').</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Confusing 'have to' with 'must'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We must wear uniforms because it's the rule.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We have to wear uniforms because it's the rule.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'have to' for external obligations or rules.</p>
</article>
</div>
