<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Adverbs</strong> add detail to verbs, adjectives, or entire sentences by indicating time, frequency, manner, degree, or attitude. This lesson helps you understand adverb placement and how they modify meaning to improve clarity and precision in your communication.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Adverbs are words that modify verbs, adjectives, or entire sentences by providing additional information about time, manner, frequency, degree, or attitude. They answer questions such as how, when, how often, and to what extent. For B2 learners, mastering adverbs enhances sentence variety and precision, making communication clearer and more effective.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing how an action is performed (e.g., "She speaks <strong>fluently</strong>").</li>
      <li>Indicating the frequency of an action (e.g., "He <strong>often</strong> visits").</li>
      <li>Specifying the time of an action (e.g., "The meeting will start <strong>soon</strong>").</li>
      <li>Expressing the degree of an adjective or another adverb (e.g., "It is <strong>very</strong> important").</li>
      <li>Conveying the speaker's attitude or opinion (e.g., "<strong>Fortunately</strong>, the rain stopped").</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>quickly</strong> finished her work before the deadline.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">He <strong>often</strong> visits his grandparents on weekends.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The meeting will start <strong>soon</strong>.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use adverbs to modify verbs, adjectives, or sentences for clarity.</li>
      <li>Place adverbs close to the words they modify to avoid confusion.</li>
      <li>Use adjective forms after linking verbs (e.g., "She seems happy").</li>
      <li>Choose the correct adverb form to match the intended meaning.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using the wrong form or placing the adverb in an awkward position</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The train arrived lately, so many workers were delayed.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The train arrived late, so many workers were delayed.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'late' as the correct adverb form to describe the timing of the train's arrival.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using an adjective after an action verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He answered quick during the interview.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He answered quickly during the interview.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'quickly' to properly modify the verb 'answered'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Placing the frequency adverb where it sounds unnatural</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>I go always by train to the city centre.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>I always go by train to the city centre.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Place 'always' before the main verb to maintain natural word order.</p>
</article>
</div>
