<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Present Continuous</strong> helps you express actions happening right now or around the current time. Mastering this tense allows you to communicate ongoing actions clearly and accurately in both written and spoken English.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She always complains about the noise.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She is always complaining about the noise.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use Present Continuous with 'always' to express annoyance at repeated actions.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>I work on the project right now.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I am working on the project right now.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Present Continuous is used for actions happening at the moment of speaking.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The <strong>Present Continuous</strong> tense is used to describe actions that are happening at the moment of speaking or around the current period. It is formed using the present tense of the verb 'to be' (am/is/are) followed by the base verb ending in -ing.</p>
    <p>This tense is crucial at the B2 level for expressing ongoing actions and for making your communication more dynamic and precise.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing actions happening now or around the current time.</li>
      <li>Talking about temporary situations.</li>
      <li>Indicating future plans or arrangements.</li>
      <li>Expressing annoyance or irritation with repeated actions using 'always'.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>I am working</strong> on the project right now.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>She is always complaining</strong> about the noise.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>They are meeting</strong> us at the restaurant at 7 PM.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'am/is/are' + verb-ing for actions happening now.</li>
      <li>Use Present Continuous for temporary actions.</li>
      <li>Include time expressions like 'now', 'at the moment', or 'currently'.</li>
      <li>Use Present Continuous for definite future arrangements.</li>
      <li>Be careful not to confuse with Present Simple for habitual actions.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using Present Simple instead of Present Continuous</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He reads a book right now.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He is reading a book right now.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use Present Continuous for actions happening at the moment.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Forgetting 'to be' in Present Continuous</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>They going to the market.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>They are going to the market.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Remember to include 'am/is/are' before the verb-ing form.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Using Present Continuous for habitual actions</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She is going to the gym every day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She goes to the gym every day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use Present Simple for habitual actions.</p>
</article>
</div>
