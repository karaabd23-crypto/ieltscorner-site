<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Academic Verbs</strong> are essential for effectively communicating complex ideas and arguments in scholarly writing. This lesson focuses on using precise verbs to describe, analyze, and interpret information accurately.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>The chart says bus use went up in May.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The chart <strong>indicates</strong> that bus use rose in May.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger version uses a precise verb to convey the data accurately.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>The writer says there is a reason for the change.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The writer <strong>explains</strong> one reason for the change.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The better sentence uses a verb that clarifies the writer's action.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Academic verbs are specific action words used to convey complex ideas and arguments in scholarly writing. They help articulate analysis, description, and interpretation of information effectively.</p>
    <p>These verbs are crucial at a B2 level and beyond, where clarity and precision are essential for academic success. Examples include <strong>analyze</strong>, <strong>evaluate</strong>, <strong>justify</strong>, and <strong>interpret</strong>.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing data or research findings in academic essays or reports.</li>
      <li>Explaining the significance of results in scholarly discussions.</li>
      <li>Justifying arguments or positions in formal writing.</li>
      <li>Interpreting evidence or trends in research articles.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The study <strong>analyzes</strong> the impact of climate change on agriculture.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The researcher <strong>justifies</strong> the need for further investigation.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Choose verbs that precisely match the action or idea you wish to convey.</li>
      <li>Pair academic verbs with evidence or examples to strengthen arguments.</li>
      <li>Vary verb choice to maintain reader interest and clarity.</li>
      <li>Avoid overusing any single verb to prevent redundancy.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using a broad everyday verb where an academic verb is more exact</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The chart says the route helped workers.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The chart <strong>indicates</strong> that the route improved commuting times for workers.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Choose a verb that precisely describes the action or data.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using an academic verb without evidence after it</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The writer evaluates the issue.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The writer <strong>evaluates</strong> the issue by comparing cost with long-term benefit.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Follow the verb with specific evidence or ideas.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Repeating the same academic verb in every sentence</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The chart shows one trend, and the writer shows one cause.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The chart <strong>indicates</strong> one trend, and the writer <strong>explains</strong> one cause.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Vary the verb to match the specific action or idea.</p>
</article>
</div>
