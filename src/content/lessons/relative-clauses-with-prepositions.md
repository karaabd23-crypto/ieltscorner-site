## Goal
Learn how to use **Relative Clauses With Prepositions** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What Relative Clauses With Prepositions Means
This lesson is specifically about **Relative Clauses With Prepositions**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
Relative Clauses With Prepositions controls relational meaning between words (time, place, direction, cause, and abstract relationships).

Use Relative Clauses With Prepositions by selecting the preposition that matches the exact relationship required by the verb, adjective, or noun pattern.

Use conditions:
- Check fixed verb/adjective + preposition combinations.
- Differentiate time and place sets (e.g., at/on/in) by precision and scale.
- Remove extra prepositions that do not carry meaning.

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate Relative Clauses With Prepositions helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When Relative Clauses With Prepositions is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Choose the exact grammar job first: where **Relative Clauses With Prepositions** controls time, relationship, or emphasis.
2. Build a complete base clause, then place **Relative Clauses With Prepositions** in the position required by the pattern.
3. Check agreement and tense around **Relative Clauses With Prepositions** before adding extra words.
4. Add one supporting clause only if the logic stays clear in public policy and daily life context.
5. Re-read for one specific risk: wrong form, wrong position, or mixed timeline.

## Real-World Examples with Relative Clauses With Prepositions
### Example 1
- Weak: *She is interested on environmental policy.*
- Strong: *She is interested in environmental policy.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *We discussed about the budget during class.*
- Strong: *We discussed the budget during class.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Relative Clauses With Prepositions
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: using incorrect or unnecessary prepositions after common verbs and adjectives</summary>

- Weak: *She is interested on environmental policy.*
- Strong: *She is interested in environmental policy.*
- Fix: learn high-frequency verb-preposition and adjective-preposition pairs.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *We discussed about the budget during class.*
- Strong: *We discussed the budget during class.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in Relative Clauses With Prepositions without improving meaning</summary>

- Weak: *The writer attempts Relative Clauses With Prepositions, but the sentence becomes difficult to follow due to unnecessary additions.*
- Strong: *The writer uses Relative Clauses With Prepositions selectively, so the sentence remains clear and focused.*
- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
</details>
