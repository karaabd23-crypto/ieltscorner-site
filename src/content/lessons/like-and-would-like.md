<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Like and would like</strong> are essential for expressing preferences and making polite requests in English. Understanding the difference helps in communicating more effectively in various contexts, such as ordering food, making requests, or discussing interests.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>I like to have some water, please.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I would like to have some water, please.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use 'would like' for polite requests, especially in formal settings.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She would like pizza every Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She likes pizza every Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use 'like' for habitual actions or general preferences.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p><strong>'Like'</strong> is used to express general preferences or enjoyment, while <strong>'would like'</strong> is used to make polite requests or express specific desires.</p>
    <p>The structure for 'like': subject + like(s) + noun/verb-ing.</p>
    <p>The structure for 'would like': subject + would like + noun/to + base verb.</p>
    <p>This distinction is important at the B2 level for nuanced communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing general preferences or habitual likes (e.g., "I like coffee.").</li>
      <li>Making polite requests (e.g., "I would like a coffee, please.").</li>
      <li>Expressing specific desires or intentions (e.g., "I would like to visit Paris.").</li>
      <li>In formal or semi-formal situations where politeness is required.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I <strong>like</strong> reading books in my free time.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>would like</strong> to order the pasta.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">We <strong>would like</strong> more information about the program.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'like' for general preferences or enjoyment.</li>
      <li>Use 'would like' for polite requests or specific desires.</li>
      <li>Remember: 'would like' is more formal than 'like'.</li>
      <li>Don't confuse 'like' with 'would like' in formal requests.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Confusing 'like' with 'would like' in requests</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>I like a cup of tea, please.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>I would like a cup of tea, please.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'would like' for polite requests.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using 'would like' for general preferences</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He would like chocolate.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He likes chocolate.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'like' for general preferences.</p>
