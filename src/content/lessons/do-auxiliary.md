<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Do</strong> functions as an auxiliary verb to form questions, negatives, and add emphasis in English sentences. Mastering its use is essential for clear and grammatically correct communication at the B2 level.</p></div>

  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Does he know the answer?</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Does' is the correct auxiliary verb for forming questions with third-person singular subjects.</p>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The auxiliary verb <strong>do</strong> is used to form questions, negatives, and add emphasis in English sentences. It helps to clarify the action or state described by the main verb. For example, in questions, <strong>do</strong> assists in inverting the subject and verb order. At the B2 level, mastering the use of <strong>do</strong> enhances clarity and grammatical accuracy in both spoken and written English.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Forming questions in the present simple or past simple tense (e.g., "Do you like coffee?").</li>
      <li>Creating negative sentences in the present simple or past simple tense (e.g., "She does not agree.").</li>
      <li>Adding emphasis to affirmative sentences (e.g., "I do understand your point.").</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Do</strong> you need any help with your project?</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>does</strong> not like spicy food.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I <strong>do</strong> appreciate your assistance.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>do</strong> or <strong>does</strong> for questions and negatives in the present simple tense.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>did</strong> for questions and negatives in the past simple tense.</li>
      <li>Remember to match <strong>do</strong> or <strong>does</strong> with the subject (I/you/we/they use "do"; he/she/it uses "does").</li>
      <li>Use <strong>do</strong> for emphasis in affirmative sentences to strengthen the statement.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Incorrect use of 'do' with third-person singular</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He do not understand the final instruction.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He does not understand the final instruction.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'does' for third-person singular subjects in negative sentences.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Incorrect question formation with 'do'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Do she like the movie?</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Does she like the movie?</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'does' to form questions with third-person singular subjects.</p>
</article>
</div>
