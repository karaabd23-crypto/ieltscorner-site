CELPIP Task 1 Email Tone Control helps you write a complete, organized CELPIP Task 1 response. You will see how to structure your answer, which language patterns score highest, and which errors to avoid.

## Examples

Compare these two responses for the same prompt. Notice what makes the second one score higher.

<div class="lesson-grid">
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Weak response</h3>
    <p>There are many advantages and disadvantages. I think it is good because people can do many things. Also it is bad because of some problems.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Stronger response</h3>
    <p>While remote working offers employees greater schedule flexibility, it also reduces the spontaneous collaboration that drives innovation. On balance, a hybrid model addresses both concerns more effectively than either extreme.</p>
  </div>
</div>

**What changed:** The stronger response names a specific trade-off, uses a precise contrast structure, and reaches a supported position. It does not repeat generic ideas.

## How It Works

<PatternTable
  caption="Core patterns for Task 1"
  rows={[
    { pattern: "Concession + main point", meaning: "Acknowledge one side before stating your position", example: "While costs are significant, the long-term benefits outweigh them." },
    { pattern: "Specific support", meaning: "Follow every claim with a concrete example or statistic", example: "In countries such as Finland, this approach reduced dropout rates by 15%." },
    { pattern: "Controlled conclusion", meaning: "Restate your position without adding new ideas", example: "For these reasons, I believe targeted investment is the most viable solution." }
  ]}
/>

<section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
  <h3>Use these structures when</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>You need to take a clear position on a two-sided topic.</li>
    <li>You want to show range by acknowledging a counter-point before your argument.</li>
    <li>You are concluding a paragraph and need to connect back to the main claim.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Errors that cost band points">
1. Starting body paragraphs with "There are many..." -- it delays your main point.
2. Using "also" to start a new sentence -- use "In addition" or restructure.
3. Writing a conclusion that introduces new arguments -- only restate, do not add.
</Callout>

| Weak | Stronger | Fix |
|------|----------|-----|
| "There are many reasons why..." | "The primary reason for this trend is..." | Name the reason immediately. |
| "Also, it is important..." | "Furthermore, research suggests that..." | Use a formal connector. |
| "In conclusion, I think..." | "In conclusion, the evidence indicates that..." | Avoid "I think" in formal conclusions. |

## Why It Matters

Examiners score CELPIP writing on four equal criteria: Task Achievement, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy. The patterns in this lesson directly affect all four. Controlling your paragraph structure (Task Achievement), using precise connectors (Coherence), choosing accurate collocations (Lexical Resource), and maintaining consistent grammar (GRA) together determine whether you stay at Band 6 or move into Band 7+.
