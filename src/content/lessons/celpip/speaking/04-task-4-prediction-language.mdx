## What this lesson covers

CELPIP Speaking Task 4: Prediction Language gives you a clear strategy for CELPIP Task 4. You will see how strong answers are structured, which language signals score well, and what to do when ideas pause.

## Examples

<div class="lesson-grid">
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Weak answer</h3>
    <p>"Um... I think it is good. Yes. Many people like it because... um... it is nice and comfortable. I don't know... that's all."</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Stronger answer</h3>
    <p>"I would say the main advantage is flexibility. For example, people who work remotely can adjust their schedule around personal commitments, which reduces stress significantly. That said, the lack of in-person contact can affect team cohesion."</p>
  </div>
</div>

**What changed:** The stronger answer opens with a clear point, uses a specific example, and adds a counter-point. It sounds natural, not memorized.

## How It Works

<PatternTable
  caption="Reliable speaking patterns"
  rows={[
    { pattern: "Point + example", meaning: "Give your main idea, then illustrate it", example: "I enjoy cooking. For instance, I make my own bread every weekend." },
    { pattern: "Contrast signal", meaning: "Show you can see multiple sides", example: "That said, online learning does have limitations for practical subjects." },
    { pattern: "Recovery phrase", meaning: "Keep speaking when ideas pause", example: "What I mean is... / Let me think about that for a second..." }
  ]}
/>

<section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
  <h3>Key rules</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Open with your main point, not with "I think that is a good question."</li>
    <li>Give one concrete example per main idea.</li>
    <li>Use recovery language naturally -- it does not lower your score.</li>
    <li>End with a brief conclusion or counter-point to show range.</li>
  </ul>
</section>

## Common Mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="What holds scores back">
1. Starting every answer with "I think..." -- vary your openers.
2. Stopping after one sentence -- always extend with an example or reason.
3. Memorized phrases that sound unnatural -- adapt patterns, don't recite them.
</Callout>

The patterns in this lesson directly improve Fluency (because you have a structure to follow) and Lexical Resource (because you use specific vocabulary instead of vague filler). Even one additional Band point in speaking can change your overall score by 0.5.
