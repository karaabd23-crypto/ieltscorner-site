<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">In CELPIP Listening, you will miss something. The question is whether you recover or spiral. This lesson gives you a three-step recovery system that gets you back on track without losing the rest of the recording.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Accept and mark</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Write a question mark for the missed detail and keep listening immediately</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"$?M by March" -- the number is marked as unknown; the date and context are captured</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Context inference</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Use surrounding details to narrow the answer when returning to missed detail</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">If the category is "small budget increase" and options are $2M, $10M, $50M -- $2M is the inference.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Move forward, not backward</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Do not re-play the missed segment mentally -- continue with what is being said now</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">The next 30 seconds of audio contain new information worth more marks than recovering one missed detail.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Best guess is always better than blank</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Use process of elimination to narrow to the most likely answer</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">If three options are clearly wrong from context, select the fourth even without direct evidence.</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Mental replay loop</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I kept trying to remember the missed detail in my head while the speaker continued.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I wrote "?" and immediately returned attention to the current speaker.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Mental replay is silent attention cost. Every second spent replaying is a new detail missed.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Skipping context inference</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I left the question blank because I did not hear the exact answer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I narrowed the answer from two to one using the budget context and surrounding numbers.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Context inference is not guessing -- it is using available evidence to narrow uncertainty.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Stopping all note-taking</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>After missing a detail, I stopped writing notes because I was flustered.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>After writing "?", I continued the note-taking system as normal.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> The note-taking system exists precisely for moments of stress. Stopping it removes the support you need most.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">Every CELPIP Listening candidate misses a detail. The difference between CLB 8 and CLB 9 is not whether you miss something -- it is whether you recover. The mark-and-continue system (write "?", keep listening, use context inference) prevents one missed detail from becoming a cascade. Candidates who spiral after a miss consistently underperform relative to their actual comprehension ability because the recovery failure costs more marks than the original miss.</p>
