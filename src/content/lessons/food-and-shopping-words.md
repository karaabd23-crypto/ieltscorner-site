<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Essential Vocabulary for Food and Shopping</strong> focuses on the key words and phrases needed for effective communication in everyday shopping and dining scenarios. This lesson helps you understand and use these terms naturally in context.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>The market sold good food at cheap prices.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The market sold fresh produce at budget-friendly prices.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger version specifies the type of food and uses more precise language.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>The shop did not have the item, so we bought something else.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The item was out of stock, so we chose an alternative ingredient.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The improved sentence uses specific shopping vocabulary like "out of stock."</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Essential vocabulary for food and shopping includes words and phrases that describe food items, shopping experiences, and related transactions.</p>
    <p>These terms help you communicate effectively when discussing purchases, ingredients, and dining experiences.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering this vocabulary allows for more precise and natural conversations in everyday situations.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing food items and their characteristics in detail.</li>
      <li>Discussing shopping experiences, such as finding bargains or dealing with out-of-stock items.</li>
      <li>Engaging in conversations about dining experiences and preferences.</li>
      <li>Writing reviews or reports on food and shopping experiences.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The <strong>supermarket</strong> offers a wide range of <strong>organic produce</strong> at competitive prices.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">We decided to try a new <strong>restaurant</strong> that specializes in <strong>locally-sourced ingredients</strong>.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use specific terms like "receipt" instead of generic words like "paper."</li>
      <li>Pair food words with relevant contexts, like "fresh produce" with "market."</li>
      <li>Ensure the vocabulary matches the situation, such as "budget-friendly" for cost discussions.</li>
      <li>Maintain consistency in tone and formality according to the context.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using broad daily-life words instead of the exact shopping term</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The store gave us paper after payment.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The store gave us a receipt after payment.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> choose the standard term for the shopping situation</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using a food word without a real buying or cooking context</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Fresh produce is nice.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Fresh produce is cheaper at the weekend market.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> show where, why, or how the food term matters</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using a topic word in the wrong meaning</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The ingredient was budget-friendly and tasty.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The meal was budget-friendly because the ingredients were cheap and local.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> match the word to the part of the situation it actually describes</p>
</article>
</div>
