<div class="lesson-context">
  <p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Adverbs of intensity</strong> such as 'very', 'really', and 'quite' are crucial for modifying adjectives and adverbs to express varying degrees of intensity. This lesson focuses on their specific use and placement in sentences to enhance clarity and precision.</p>
</div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The weather is very cold today.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The weather is <strong>really</strong> cold today.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Really' adds more emphasis than 'very', making the statement stronger.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The exam was quite easy for everyone.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The exam was <strong>very</strong> easy for everyone.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Very' indicates a higher degree of ease than 'quite', though 'quite' is not incorrect.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>'Very', 'really', and 'quite' are adverbs that modify adjectives and adverbs to express varying degrees of intensity. They help convey how intense or to what extent a quality is present in a sentence. Mastery of these adverbs at the B2 level enhances clarity and expressiveness.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing strong feelings or opinions (e.g., 'really happy').</li>
      <li>Describing significant differences (e.g., 'very different').</li>
      <li>Softening statements to be more polite (e.g., 'quite nice').</li>
      <li>Adding emphasis in informal conversations (e.g., 'really tired').</li>
      <li>Enhancing descriptions in academic writing (e.g., 'very effective').</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The project was <strong>very</strong> successful.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">He was <strong>really</strong> impressed by the presentation.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The solution was <strong>quite</strong> innovative.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'very' to increase intensity; it is stronger than 'quite'.</li>
      <li>'Really' can replace 'very' for added emphasis, especially in informal contexts.</li>
      <li>'Quite' can mean 'fairly' or 'completely', depending on context and tone.</li>
      <li>Consider the context and tone when choosing between these adverbs.</li>
      <li>Overusing these adverbs can reduce their impact; use them judiciously.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Choosing 'very' when 'really' is more suitable for stronger emphasis</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The movie was very exciting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The movie was really exciting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'really' for stronger emphasis in informal contexts.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using 'quite' when 'very' is needed for stronger intensity</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The presentation was quite impressive.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The presentation was very impressive.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'very' to convey a higher degree of intensity.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Misinterpreting 'quite' as stronger than 'very'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He was quite certain about the decision.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He was very certain about the decision.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Remember 'very' often indicates a stronger degree than 'quite'.</p>
</article>
</div>
