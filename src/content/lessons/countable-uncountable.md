<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Countable and Uncountable Nouns</strong> are essential for expressing quantities accurately in English. Understanding the difference helps you choose the right words to convey precise amounts, whether dealing with items you can count or substances and concepts that you cannot.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>There is many information available on the topic.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>There is much information available on the topic.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Information' is uncountable, so 'much' is the correct quantifier.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She has a few money left after shopping.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She has a little money left after shopping.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Money' is uncountable, so 'a little' is the correct quantifier.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Countable nouns are items that can be counted individually, like 'apple' or 'car'. Uncountable nouns refer to substances or concepts that cannot be counted individually, such as 'water' or 'information'. This distinction is crucial for choosing the correct quantifiers and verbs.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering this distinction is essential for clarity in both written and spoken communication, especially in academic and professional contexts.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing quantities in essays or reports.</li>
      <li>Ensuring correct verb agreement in complex sentences.</li>
      <li>Using quantifiers like 'many', 'much', 'few', or 'little'.</li>
      <li>Writing formal letters or emails requiring precision.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">There are <strong>many</strong> <strong>books</strong> on the shelf.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">We have <strong>little</strong> <strong>information</strong> about the new policy.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'many' and 'few' with countable nouns.</li>
      <li>Use 'much' and 'little' with uncountable nouns.</li>
      <li>Always check if the noun can be counted before choosing a quantifier.</li>
      <li>Some nouns can be both, depending on context (e.g., 'chicken' as an animal vs. 'chicken' as food).</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using a countable quantifier with an uncountable noun</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We do not have many equipment for the science room.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We do not have much equipment for the science room.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'much' with uncountable nouns like 'equipment'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Confusing few and a few</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>A little students asked for extra practice after class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>A few students asked for extra practice after class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'a few' with countable plural nouns like 'students'.</p>
</article>
</div>
