<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Adverbs of Time and Duration</strong> provide essential details about when and for how long actions occur. Mastering their use enhances sentence clarity and precision, particularly at the B2 level, where nuanced expression is key.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She will arrive at the airport yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She arrived at the airport yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Yesterday' indicates a past action, so the verb must be in the past tense.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>They have been studying since two hours.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>They have been studying for two hours.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'For' is used with durations, while 'since' is used with specific starting points.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Adverbs of time and duration specify when an action occurs and for how long it lasts. They enhance clarity by answering 'when?' and 'for how long?', crucial for nuanced expression at the B2 level.</p>
    <p>Common adverbs include 'yesterday', 'tomorrow', 'for two hours', and 'since Monday'.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Indicating a specific time, such as 'yesterday' or 'next week'.</li>
      <li>Describing duration, like 'for three hours' or 'since last year'.</li>
      <li>Sequencing events, using phrases like 'before lunch' or 'after the meeting'.</li>
      <li>In formal writing to ensure precise timing and duration.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She will arrive <strong>tomorrow</strong>.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They have been working <strong>since morning</strong>.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The project lasted <strong>for three months</strong>.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use adverbs of time to specify when an action happens.</li>
      <li>Use adverbs of duration to indicate how long an action lasts.</li>
      <li>Place adverbs of time and duration at the beginning or end of a sentence for emphasis.</li>
      <li>Use 'for' with durations and 'since' with specific starting points.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using 'since' with durations instead of starting points</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>They have been here since two hours.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>They have been here for two hours.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'for' with durations and 'since' with starting points.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Misplacing adverbs of time in a sentence</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Tomorrow, I will finish the report.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>I will finish the report tomorrow.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Place adverbs of time at the end of a sentence for natural emphasis.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Using 'ago' with present perfect tense</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She has left two days ago.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She left two days ago.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'ago' with simple past tense, not present perfect.</p>
</article>
</div>
