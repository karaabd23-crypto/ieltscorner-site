<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Using 'Should', 'Ought To', and 'Must' for Advice</strong> helps you express varying levels of advice and obligation. These modals allow you to convey how strongly you recommend or require an action to be taken. In this lesson, you will learn how to choose the right modal for your intended message.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>'Should', 'ought to', and 'must' are modal verbs used to give advice or express obligation. They help convey the strength of your recommendation or requirement. 'Should' and 'ought to' suggest advice or mild obligation, while 'must' indicates a strong necessity or obligation.</p>
    <p>Formation: modal + base verb (e.g., should go, must see).</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, understanding the nuances between these modals is crucial for effective communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Giving advice or recommendations (e.g., "You should see a doctor.")</li>
      <li>Expressing mild obligation or expectation (e.g., "You ought to finish your homework.")</li>
      <li>Indicating strong necessity or rules (e.g., "You must wear a seatbelt.")</li>
      <li>Writing formal letters or reports where recommendations are needed.</li>
      <li>In spoken responses during exams or interviews to suggest actions.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Should</strong> you need assistance, please call us.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">You <strong>ought to</strong> apologize for the mistake.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Employees <strong>must</strong> adhere to the company policy.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'should' for advice or suggestions.</li>
      <li>'Ought to' is similar to 'should' but slightly more formal.</li>
      <li>Use 'must' for strong obligations or rules.</li>
      <li>Never add 'to' after 'should' or 'must'.</li>
      <li>Choose the modal based on the strength of advice or obligation you wish to convey.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using 'to' after a modal verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>You must to submit the form before Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>You must submit the form before Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the base verb directly after 'must'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>confusing modal strength</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She must consider the offer, but it's not a requirement.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She should consider the offer, but it's not a requirement.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'should' for advice, not 'must'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using 'ought to' incorrectly</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He ought to goes to the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He ought to go to the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> 'Ought to' should be followed by the base verb.</p>
</article>
</div>
