## Intro
Inference questions test your ability to read implied meaning. CLB 6 readers often lose marks by adding outside assumptions.

## Examiner Criteria
- Inference is grounded in textual clues.
- Selected answer is the best supported interpretation.
- Personal opinion is not used as evidence.

## Mistakes
- Choosing what "sounds true" in real life.
- Ignoring tone markers such as "however", "despite", and "likely".
- Confusing direct fact questions with inference questions.

## Strategy
1. Underline two textual clues before looking at options.
2. Convert clues into one short inference sentence.
3. Pick the option that matches your evidence sentence most closely.

## Example
Clue 1: "The manager repeatedly delayed implementation."
Clue 2: "Staff confidence dropped over three months."

Strong inference: "The delays reduced team confidence."

## Exercise
1. Take three inference questions from a practice set.
2. Write two clues and one inference sentence for each.
3. Compare your inference sentence with the correct answer.
