<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Softening Claims Carefully</strong> helps your grammar sound more precise and suitable for formal contexts. This lesson teaches you how to use language that sounds controlled and credible, ensuring your statements are balanced and persuasive.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>I think this plan is kind of bad for cities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>This plan is <strong>unlikely to benefit</strong> cities in the long term.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The revised sentence uses "unlikely to benefit" to express doubt and soften the claim.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>People absolutely hate the change, and it is a total disaster.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Many residents <strong>appear dissatisfied</strong> with the change.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The improved sentence uses "appear dissatisfied" to moderate the strength of the claim.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Softening claims involves using language that makes statements sound more tentative and balanced. This helps in making your writing or speech appear more credible and less absolute.</p>
    <p>It is formed by using hedging expressions, modal verbs, and careful word choice to moderate the strength of your claims.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering this skill is crucial for writing and speaking tasks that require a formal tone.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Writing academic essays or reports where evidence is not conclusive.</li>
      <li>Engaging in discussions where opinions need to be presented diplomatically.</li>
      <li>Drafting formal emails or letters to convey suggestions or feedback.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">This <strong>suggests</strong> that the policy <strong>may be</strong> effective in the short term.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">A more practical approach <strong>would be</strong> to improve route frequency before expansion.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use modal verbs like "might," "could," or "may" to soften statements.</li>
      <li>Incorporate phrases like "it seems," "it appears," or "this suggests" to hedge claims.</li>
      <li>Avoid absolute terms unless you have strong evidence to support them.</li>
      <li>Replace vague intensifiers with precise language to maintain credibility.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using casual or dramatic grammar in a task that needs measured formal English</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This idea is super good because everybody will love it.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>This idea <strong>could be effective</strong> because it addresses a common local need.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Replace spoken-style wording with precise, supportable claims and more controlled sentence framing.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using spoken intensifiers in a formal academic sentence</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This is a really big problem that totally affects everyone.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>This is a <strong>serious problem</strong> that affects many residents.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Replace emotional intensifiers with precise, supportable wording.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>making an absolute claim that the evidence cannot support</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This proves public transport is always the best solution.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>This <strong>suggests</strong> public transport can be the more effective solution in many cities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use measured grammar when the evidence supports a tendency, not an absolute fact.</p>
</article>
</div>
