<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">Upgrading vocabulary in CELPIP Writing is not about using longer words. It is about replacing vague, overused words with precise, contextually accurate alternatives. This lesson targets the upgrades that most reliably move a response from CLB 8 to CLB 9.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Replace "very + adjective"</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Use a stronger single adjective instead</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"very tired" → "exhausted"; "very important" → "critical"</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Replace "get/got"</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Use a precise verb that shows how or why</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"got a promotion" → "earned a promotion"; "got worse" → "deteriorated"</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Replace "thing/issue/problem"</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Name the specific noun</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"the main issue" → "the key barrier"; "the thing is" → "the core challenge is"</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Replace "big/small"</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Use a measurable or context-specific adjective</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"a big difference" → "a significant difference"; "small amount" → "minimal uptake"</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Vague intensifier</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>This is a very serious and very important problem.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>This is a critical, wide-reaching problem.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Stack two precise adjectives instead of repeating "very".</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Overused verb</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>We got a lot of useful feedback from the survey.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>We gathered substantial feedback through the survey.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> "Gathered" signals intentional collection; "substantial" replaces "a lot of".</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Vague noun</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>There are many things that need to change about this policy.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Several provisions in this policy require amendment.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Name the specific noun (provisions) and use a formal verb (require amendment).</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Vocabulary Range at CLB 9 rewards candidates who use precise, context-appropriate language. The most accessible upgrade path is not learning obscure words -- it is replacing the ten most overused vague words in your writing (very, thing, get, big, good, bad, nice, a lot, important, problem) with accurate alternatives you already know but are not yet deploying in exam conditions.</p>
