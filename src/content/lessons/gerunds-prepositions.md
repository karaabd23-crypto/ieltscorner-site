<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Gerunds After Prepositions</strong> focuses on using the -ing form of verbs after prepositions to accurately express actions. This lesson will help you confidently choose the correct form, enhancing your grammatical precision in both writing and speaking.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>He is interested to join the evening class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>He is interested in joining the evening class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The gerund 'joining' follows the preposition 'in' correctly.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She apologized to be late.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She apologized for being late.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Being' is the correct gerund form following the preposition 'for'.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Using a gerund (-ing form) after a preposition indicates an action related to the preposition. This structure is essential for expressing nuanced relationships between actions and their contexts, enhancing grammatical accuracy and fluency at the B2 level.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>After prepositions such as 'in', 'on', 'at', 'with', 'about', 'for', etc.</li>
      <li>In formal writing and speaking to ensure grammatical correctness.</li>
      <li>When describing actions linked to specific nouns or pronouns.</li>
      <li>When constructing complex sentences that involve multiple clauses.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She is interested <strong>in learning</strong> new languages.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They talked <strong>about going</strong> to the conference.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Always use the -ing form of a verb after a preposition.</li>
      <li>Memorize common preposition and gerund combinations.</li>
      <li>Ensure the action described is logically connected to the preposition.</li>
      <li>Be cautious of verbs that change meaning when switching between gerunds and infinitives.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using the wrong form after a preposition</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He is excited about to travel.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He is excited about travelling.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the gerund form after prepositions.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>forgetting the gerund after prepositions</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>They are committed to improve the process.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>They are committed to improving the process.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Remember that 'to' as a preposition requires a gerund.</p>
</article>
</div>
