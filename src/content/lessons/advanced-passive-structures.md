<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Advanced Passive Voice</strong> changes the focus of the sentence. Sometimes the action matters more than the doer. In this lesson, you will see when passive voice helps and when active voice is still the better choice.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The passive is mainly a focus choice: who matters more, the doer or the result.</p>
<p>The passive changes the focus of the sentence from the doer to the action or result. It is formed with be plus a past participle.</p>
<p>Use the passive when the action matters more than the doer, when the doer is unknown, or when naming the doer adds nothing useful.</p>
<p>At C1 level, use this structure for precision and logic, not for decorative complexity.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul><li>Build the passive with the correct form of be and the past participle.</li><li>Name the agent with by only when the reader needs that detail.</li><li>Switch back to active voice if the passive hides responsibility or makes the sentence weaker.</li></ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack"><div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">More buses were added to the route last winter.</div>
<div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The final timetable will be announced on Friday.</div></div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul><li>Ask what should receive the focus: the doer or the action/result.</li><li>Build the passive with be + past participle and keep the tense accurate.</li><li>Add the agent with by only when it gives useful information.</li><li>Switch back to active voice if responsibility becomes unclear.</li><li>Read the finished sentence once to make sure it still sounds natural.</li></ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using an active verb form where passive focus is required</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The final report publish next Monday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The final report will be published next Monday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> use be + past participle and keep tense accurate</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>forgetting the auxiliary verb in a passive sentence</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The final decision announced yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The final decision was announced yesterday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> passive forms need a form of be plus the past participle</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using passive voice when the active sentence is clearer</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Mistakes were made by the planning team during the launch.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The planning team made mistakes during the launch.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> switch back to active voice when responsibility matters</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance"><strong>&#127919; Why it matters:</strong> this topic affects clarity more than many learners expect. A small grammar change can make the sentence more accurate, more natural, and easier for the examiner or listener to trust. Strong control of <u>Advanced Passive Voice</u> helps your message stay clear from start to finish.</p>
