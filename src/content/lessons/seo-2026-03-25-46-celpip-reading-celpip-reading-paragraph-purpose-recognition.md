<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">Some CELPIP Reading questions ask about the purpose of a paragraph or section rather than a specific fact. Recognising the five paragraph purposes quickly eliminates wrong answers and speeds up response time.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Introduce (background)</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">The paragraph sets up context before the main argument</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Starts with history, statistics, or definitions. No position is taken yet.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Argue (position)</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">The paragraph states a claim and supports it</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Contains "therefore", "as a result", "this shows that", or a clear recommendation</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Illustrate (example)</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">The paragraph provides a case study or example to support a previous claim</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"For example, in 2022, a Calgary hospital..." -- pure example, no new claim</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Concede (counter-argument)</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">The paragraph acknowledges the opposing view before refuting it</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Starts with "While some argue..." or "Critics contend that..." then pivots with "however"</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Conclude (summary)</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">The paragraph restates the main position and closes the argument</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Contains "in summary", "therefore", "ultimately" with no new evidence</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Confusing illustration with argument</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>The paragraph gives an example so it must be supporting a claim.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The paragraph gives an example -- its purpose is to illustrate, not to argue. The argument was in the previous paragraph.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> An example paragraph does not contain a new claim. If it only has a case study or anecdote, its purpose is illustration.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Missing the concession pivot</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>The paragraph starts with the opposing view so it must be against the author's position.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The paragraph starts with the opposing view (concede) and then pivots with "however" to support the author's argument.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Look for "however", "but", "despite this" after the opposing view to identify a concession paragraph.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Naming content not purpose</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>The paragraph's purpose is to talk about hospital funding.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The paragraph's purpose is to introduce the problem of funding gaps before the author's policy argument.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Paragraph purpose is structural (introduce, argue, illustrate, concede, conclude), not topical.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Reading paragraph-purpose questions are structural, not topical. The fastest and most reliable strategy is to read the first sentence (to identify the opening move) and look for connective words (to confirm the role). The five-category framework -- introduce, argue, illustrate, concede, conclude -- covers all question options you will encounter. Candidates who memorise this framework and apply it structurally rather than topically answer purpose questions in under 60 seconds.</p>
