<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Prepositions in Context</strong> focuses on using prepositions accurately in phrases and sentences to convey clear and natural meaning in English. This lesson helps you master common collocations and fixed expressions involving prepositions, enhancing your fluency and precision.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>She is interested on environmental policy.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She is interested in environmental policy.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The preposition 'in' correctly follows 'interested' to indicate what she is interested in.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>We arrived to the station on time.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>We arrived at the station on time.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The preposition 'at' is used with 'arrive' to indicate a specific location.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Prepositions are words that show the relationship between a noun or pronoun and other parts of a sentence. They often indicate time, place, direction, or introduce objects.</p>
    <p>In this lesson, you will learn how to use prepositions in common collocations and fixed expressions to convey clear meaning.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, understanding these prepositions in context is essential for accurate and natural English communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing relationships in time (e.g., on Monday, at 5 PM).</li>
      <li>Indicating location or direction (e.g., at the station, to the office).</li>
      <li>Using fixed expressions and collocations (e.g., interested in, responsible for).</li>
      <li>Clarifying actions with verbs that require specific prepositions (e.g., depend on, arrive at).</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She is <strong>interested in</strong> environmental policy.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">We <strong>arrived at</strong> the station on time.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'in' for months, years, and long periods (e.g., in 2020).</li>
      <li>Use 'at' for specific times and places (e.g., at 3 PM, at the corner).</li>
      <li>Use 'on' for days and dates (e.g., on Monday, on July 4th).</li>
      <li>Learn prepositions as part of collocations (e.g., good at, afraid of).</li>
      <li>Avoid translating prepositions directly from your native language.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Translating a preposition directly from another language</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He depends of his parents for advice.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He depends on his parents for advice.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Learn the full English collocation 'depend on' instead of translating word-for-word.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Adding a preposition after a verb that does not need one</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We discussed about the timetable after class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We discussed the timetable after class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Remember that 'discuss' does not require a preposition.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Mixing time and place prepositions</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The interview starts in Monday at the office.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The interview starts on Monday at the office.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'on' for days and 'at' for locations.</p>
</article>
</div>
