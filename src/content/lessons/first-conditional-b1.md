<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>First conditional</strong> talks about a real future possibility. It shows what may happen if one condition happens first. In this lesson, you will learn the form, the meaning, and the mistakes students make most often.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>If governments invest in transit, traffic reduced quickly.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>If governments invest in transit, traffic will reduce quickly.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The correction uses "will" to indicate a future result, aligning with the first conditional structure.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>If students will review tonight, they will feel calmer tomorrow.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>If students review tonight, they will feel calmer tomorrow.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Will" is removed from the if-clause to follow the correct first conditional form.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The <strong>first conditional</strong> describes a real or likely situation in the future. It expresses what will occur if a specific condition is met. The structure is: <strong>if + present simple, then will + base verb</strong>. Mastery at the B2 level is essential for discussing future possibilities accurately.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Discussing future plans or predictions based on a condition.</li>
      <li>Giving warnings about future consequences.</li>
      <li>Making promises contingent on a specific condition.</li>
      <li>Explaining likely outcomes in formal or informal settings.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">If you <strong>study</strong> hard, you <strong>will pass</strong> the exam.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">If it <strong>rains</strong> tomorrow, we <strong>will cancel</strong> the picnic.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Always use the present simple tense after "if".</li>
      <li>Use "will" in the main clause to indicate a future result.</li>
      <li>Avoid using "will" in the if-clause.</li>
      <li>Ensure the condition and result are realistic and possible.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Breaking the normal first conditional pattern</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>If students study consistently, they would improve their scores quickly.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>If students study consistently, they will improve their scores quickly.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use present simple after "if" and a future result clause.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Putting will inside a standard if-clause</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>If students will revise regularly, they will improve faster.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>If students revise regularly, they will improve faster.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use present simple in the if-clause for first conditional.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Using would in the result of a real future possibility</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>If the bus arrives on time, I would get to class early.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>If the bus arrives on time, I will get to class early.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "will" for the likely future result, not "would".</p>
</article>
</div>
