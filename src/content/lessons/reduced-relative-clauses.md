<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Short Relative Clauses</strong> enable you to incorporate essential details about a noun within the same sentence, streamlining your writing and speech. This lesson will guide you in using them effectively to enhance clarity and detail in your communication.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The book she wrote became a bestseller.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The book <strong>she wrote</strong> became a bestseller.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The relative pronoun 'that' is omitted because it is not the subject of the clause.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The project we completed was successful.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The project <strong>we completed</strong> was successful.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The sentence is more concise without the relative pronoun 'that'.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Short relative clauses are a type of relative clause that omits the relative pronoun when it is not the subject of the clause. They provide essential or additional information about a noun without forming a separate sentence.</p>
    <p>They allow for more concise and fluid sentences by integrating additional details directly into the main clause.</p>
    <p>This skill is important at the B2 level as it enhances sentence variety and complexity, which are evaluated in writing and speaking tasks.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>You want to add essential information about a noun without a separate sentence.</li>
      <li>The relative pronoun is not the subject of the clause.</li>
      <li>In writing tasks where sentence variety and conciseness are valued.</li>
      <li>In spoken responses to quickly provide additional details.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The book <strong>she wrote</strong> became a bestseller.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The project <strong>we completed</strong> was successful.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Omit the relative pronoun if it is not the subject of the clause.</li>
      <li>Ensure the clause directly follows the noun it describes.</li>
      <li>Use short relative clauses to make sentences more concise.</li>
      <li>Avoid using short relative clauses if clarity is compromised.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Including unnecessary relative pronouns</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The book that she wrote became a bestseller.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The book <strong>she wrote</strong> became a bestseller.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Omit 'that' when it is not the subject of the clause.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using short clauses incorrectly</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The report which we submitted was approved.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The report <strong>we submitted</strong> was approved.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Remove 'which' to make the sentence more concise.</p>
</article>
</div>
