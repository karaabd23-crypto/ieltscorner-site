<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">Inference questions are the hardest question type in CELPIP Reading because the answer is not stated directly in the passage. This lesson gives you a three-step strategy to identify implicit meaning without guessing.</p></div>

  <p class="lesson-card-label">Stronger</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>"The passage says the company 'paused new hires indefinitely'. The question asks what this implies about the company's finances. The phrase 'paused indefinitely' implies financial uncertainty, not a planned restructure."</p>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Locate the relevant passage section</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Find the two or three sentences that the question references</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Question: "What does the author imply about the policy?" Locate the paragraph where the policy is discussed.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Read for what is NOT said</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Look at what is implied by word choice, not just what is stated</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"Paused indefinitely" implies uncertainty; "temporarily suspended" implies a planned return. The distinction is the inference.</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Eliminate extreme options first</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Inference answers are usually moderate. Reject options with "always", "never", "completely"</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">If an option says "the author completely opposes the policy", look for softer phrasing like "the author expresses concern".</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Test each option against the text</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Go back to the passage and verify each option is supported, not just plausible</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">An option may sound reasonable but is not actually supported by the passage text.</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Using outside knowledge</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I chose the answer because it is what usually happens in this kind of situation.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I chose the answer because it is the only option supported by the passage wording.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> CELPIP inference questions test what the text implies, not what is true in general. Outside knowledge can lead to selecting a plausible but unsupported option.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Choosing the most extreme option</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I chose "the author strongly opposes all government intervention" because the author criticised one policy.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The author criticised the specific policy; the supported inference is "the author has reservations about this approach".</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Inference answers almost never include absolute language. If an option has "always", "never", "all", or "completely", it is almost always wrong.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Skipping back to the passage</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>I answered the inference question from memory without returning to the text.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>I returned to the two sentences referenced by the question and checked each option against them.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Inference questions require close reading of a short section. Memory alone is unreliable for word-choice nuance.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP inference questions test whether you can read between the lines of a specific text, not whether you have general knowledge or can identify repeated vocabulary. The three-step strategy -- locate, read for implication, eliminate and verify -- converts the most difficult question type into a structured process that does not depend on instinct or guessing. Candidates who consistently apply this strategy see the most improvement in their Reading score with the fewest questions remaining unanswered.</p>
