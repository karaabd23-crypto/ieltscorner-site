<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">Part 1 questions are short and personal — about daily life, habits, preferences. Most test-takers give one-sentence answers and stop, or ramble for 30 seconds without structure. The ideal Part 1 answer is 2-3 sentences: a direct answer, a reason or detail, and an optional extension.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Weak</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>"Do you like cooking?" → "Yes, I like cooking. It is good."</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Stronger</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>"Do you like cooking?" → "Yes, quite a lot actually — I find it a good way to switch off after work. I particularly enjoy making dishes from scratch rather than following a recipe too closely."</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The strong answer gives a direct reply (yes), a reason tied to a real experience (switch off after work), and a natural extension (from scratch vs recipe). It takes about 15 seconds — long enough to show fluency, short enough not to ramble.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">The 2-3 sentence frame</p>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Answer:</strong> direct response to the question (yes/no + one word).</li>
      <li><strong>Reason/detail:</strong> why, when, how often, or a specific example.</li>
      <li><strong>Extension (optional):</strong> contrast, follow-up, or preference.</li>
    </ol>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Target length</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Yes/no questions: 2-3 sentences, 10-20 seconds.</li>
      <li>How often / When / Where: 2-3 sentences, same target.</li>
      <li>Do not ask the examiner to repeat unless you genuinely did not hear.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Useful answer starters</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Yes, <strong>quite a lot actually</strong> — [reason].</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Not really, to be honest — [reason]. I much prefer [alternative].</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">It depends, I suppose — [condition A vs condition B].</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Never repeat the question back as your first sentence.</li>
      <li>Give a reason even for simple yes/no answers.</li>
      <li>End naturally — do not trail off with "...and so on" or "...etc."</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">One-word answer</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>"Do you enjoy reading?" → "Yes."</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>"Do you enjoy reading?" → "Yes, very much — I read mostly non-fiction, especially books about history and economics. I find it a more focused way to learn than watching videos."</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Always follow yes/no with a reason and at least one specific detail.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Repeating the question</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>"Do you like sports?" → "I like sports. Sports are good for health."</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>"Do you like sports?" → "Yes, I do — I play tennis twice a week and I try to go running on weekends too."</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Start with your answer and reason, not a repetition of the question words.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">IELTS Speaking Part 1 tests whether you can communicate comfortably about familiar topics. The examiner is listening for natural fluency and appropriate length — not complexity. A 2-3 sentence answer with a clear reason demonstrates both. One-sentence answers interrupt fluency; 30-second rambles reduce coherence.</p>
