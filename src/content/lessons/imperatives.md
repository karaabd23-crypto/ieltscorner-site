<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Imperative Sentences</strong> are used for giving instructions, warnings, and direct advice. They are typically short, but must remain clear and polite enough for the context. This lesson will help you learn how to give instructions effectively and naturally, using imperative sentences.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>You open the booklet and read question one.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Open the booklet and read question one.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Imperative sentences omit the subject and use the base verb for direct instructions.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Do not to forget your ID on test day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Do not forget your ID on test day.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use the base verb without "to" for negative imperatives.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Imperative sentences are used to give commands, instructions, or advice directly. They typically use the base form of the verb and omit the subject, which is implicitly 'you'. This form is crucial at the B2 level for clear and effective communication in both spoken and written English.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Giving direct instructions or commands, such as in manuals or recipes.</li>
      <li>Offering advice or suggestions in a straightforward manner.</li>
      <li>Issuing warnings or alerts in safety notices.</li>
      <li>In informal spoken contexts to prompt action, like "Come here."</li>
      <li>When brevity and clarity are needed, such as in emergency situations.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Open</strong> the booklet and <strong>read</strong> question one.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Do not</strong> leave the answer sheet blank.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Start with the base verb to form an imperative.</li>
      <li>Omit the subject unless emphasis is needed.</li>
      <li>Use "do not" or "don't" for negative imperatives.</li>
      <li>In formal contexts, soften imperatives with "please" or choose indirect forms.</li>
      <li>Ensure instructions are concise and clear to avoid confusion.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Adding an unnecessary subject or infinitive marker</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Please to check the heading before you write.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Please check the heading before you write.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the base verb directly for the instruction.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Making the instruction longer than necessary</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>You should now carefully read the first paragraph.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Read the first paragraph carefully.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the base verb directly for a clear instruction.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Building a negative instruction incorrectly</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Not use your phone during the practice test.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Do not use your phone during the practice test.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "do not" or "don't" for negative imperatives.</p>
</article>
</div>
