<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Phrasal verbs in everyday situations</strong> are crucial for expressing actions and ideas naturally in English. These verbs combine a base verb with a particle, creating a new meaning that often cannot be deduced from the individual parts. Mastering phrasal verbs allows you to communicate more fluently and understand native speakers better.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>He decided to call it off the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>He decided to call off the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The phrasal verb 'call off' means to cancel, and the object should follow the complete verb.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She looked the information up on the internet.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She looked up the information on the internet.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Look up' is the correct order for this separable phrasal verb when the object is a noun.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Phrasal verbs are combinations of a verb and one or more particles (like 'up', 'off', 'out') that create a new meaning distinct from the original verb. They are essential for fluent communication and often appear in informal contexts.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, understanding phrasal verbs is crucial for grasping nuanced meanings in both spoken and written English.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing actions in informal or conversational English.</li>
      <li>Understanding native speakers in everyday conversations.</li>
      <li>Writing informal emails or messages.</li>
      <li>Interpreting idiomatic expressions in media and literature.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>gave up</strong> smoking last year.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">They <strong>ran into</strong> an old friend at the mall.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Learn phrasal verbs as fixed expressions.</li>
      <li>Check if the object can be placed between the verb and particle.</li>
      <li>Use phrasal verbs in informal settings for natural speech.</li>
      <li>Be aware of phrasal verbs that have multiple meanings.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Incorrect particle placement</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He turned the offer down.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He turned down the offer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Place the object after the phrasal verb for clarity.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Incorrect verb conjugation</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She gave uped on her diet.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She gave up on her diet.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Only conjugate the main verb, not the particle.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Misunderstanding phrasal verb meaning</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He broke up the meeting early.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He wrapped up the meeting early.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the correct phrasal verb to convey the intended action.</p>
</article>
</div>
