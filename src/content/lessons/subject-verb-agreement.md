<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Subject and Verb Agreement</strong> ensures that the subject and the verb in a sentence match in number. This agreement is essential for clear and precise communication, particularly in complex sentences. In this lesson, you will learn how to maintain this agreement effectively.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>A list of solutions are on the board.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>A list of solutions is on the board.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The verb 'is' agrees with the singular subject 'list', not the plural 'solutions'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The number of students have increased this year.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The number of students has increased this year.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The verb 'has' agrees with 'the number', which is singular, not 'students'.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Subject-verb agreement refers to the grammatical rule that the verb must match the subject in number and person. This ensures clarity in communication, especially in complex sentences where the subject may not be immediately obvious. At the B2 level, mastering this helps in constructing grammatically correct and sophisticated sentences.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>The subject is separated from the verb by additional phrases or clauses.</li>
      <li>Using collective nouns that can be singular or plural based on context.</li>
      <li>Constructing sentences with complex noun phrases.</li>
      <li>Writing formal documents where precision is crucial.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">A list of solutions <strong>is</strong> on the board.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The number of applicants <strong>has</strong> increased this year.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Either the manager or the employees <strong>are</strong> responsible.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Identify the true subject before selecting the verb form.</li>
      <li>Ignore intervening phrases that do not affect subject number.</li>
      <li>With either/or, neither/nor, match the verb to the nearest subject.</li>
      <li>Collective nouns may require singular or plural verbs based on context.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Matching the verb to the nearest noun instead of the true subject</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>A series of workshops were added this term.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>A series of workshops was added this term.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Identify 'series' as the singular subject, not 'workshops'.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Matching the verb to the noun after 'of'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>A range of options are available to applicants.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>A range of options is available to applicants.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Match the verb to 'range', which is singular.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Ignoring the nearer subject in either/or patterns</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Either the students or the tutor are presenting first.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Either the students or the tutor is presenting first.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> In either/or, the verb agrees with the subject closest to it.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance"><strong>&#127919; Why it matters:</strong> Subject-verb agreement is crucial for clarity and professionalism in writing. Correct agreement helps the reader easily understand who or what is performing the action, making your writing more polished and effective.</p>
