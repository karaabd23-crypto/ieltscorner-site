<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>A, An, The</strong> are small words before nouns: <em>a</em>, <em>an</em>, and <em>the</em>. They show whether something is general, one of many, or already known. In this lesson, you will learn the simple meaning behind each choice.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Articles look small, but they change what the noun means to the reader.</p>
<p>Articles tell the reader whether a noun is new, known, specific, or general. The main choices are a, an, the, or no article.</p>
<p>Use a/an for one non-specific countable noun. Use the when both writer and reader can identify the noun. Use no article for many general plural and uncountable meanings.</p>
<p>At B2 level, build one correct base sentence first, then add detail without breaking grammar control.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul><li>Choose a or an from the sound, not the spelling.</li><li>Use the for shared or already mentioned nouns.</li><li>Leave the article out when you are making a general statement about plural or uncountable nouns.</li></ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack"><div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">A student asked a question, and the teacher answered it clearly.</div>
<div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Public transport reduces traffic when the system is reliable.</div></div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul><li>Check the noun first: singular countable, plural, or uncountable.</li><li>Ask whether the noun is new information or already known to the reader.</li><li>Use a/an for one non-specific countable noun and choose by sound, not spelling.</li><li>Use the for a shared, specific, or previously mentioned noun.</li><li>Leave the article out when you are speaking generally about plural or uncountable nouns.</li></ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>omitting a, an, or the where a noun needs one</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She wants to become engineer in future.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She wants to become an engineer in the future.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> check countable singular nouns and known specific references</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using the with a general plural noun</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The bicycles are healthier than driving in cities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Bicycles are healthier than driving in cities.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> remove the when you mean things in general</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>choosing a/an from spelling instead of sound</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He waited for a hour before boarding.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He waited for an hour before boarding.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> choose a/an from sound, not the first letter</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance"><strong>&#127919; Why it matters:</strong> small words before nouns often decide whether the message sounds natural or strange. When you control <u>A, An, The</u>, your sentences become more accurate and much easier for the reader or listener to trust.</p>
