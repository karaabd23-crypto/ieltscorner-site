## Goal
Learn how to use **Would: Conditional and Habitual** accurately in IELTS and CELPIP responses.

## What Would: Conditional and Habitual Means
This lesson is specifically about **Would: Conditional and Habitual**. By the end, you should understand when to use it, how to form it correctly, and how to avoid common errors.

## Topic Explanation and Use
Would: Conditional and Habitual controls hypothetical vs real situations. The form shows whether a result is factual, probable, or imagined.

Use Would: Conditional and Habitual by matching the if-clause form to the result clause so probability and timeline stay consistent.

Use conditions:
- Choose one conditional pattern and keep both clauses aligned.
- Use would/could/might in result clauses for unreal present/future meaning.
- Avoid mixing real and unreal patterns in one condition-result pair.

Reference examples:
- *In exam writing, accurate Would helps each sentence carry one clear grammatical purpose.*
- *When Would is chosen deliberately, arguments stay precise and easier to evaluate under time pressure.*

## Key Rule in Plain Language
1. Choose the exact grammar job first: where **Would** controls time, relationship, or emphasis.
2. Build a complete base clause, then place **Would** in the position required by the pattern.
3. Check agreement and tense around **Would** before adding extra words.
4. Add one supporting clause only if the logic stays clear in public policy and daily life context.
5. Re-read for one specific risk: wrong form, wrong position, or mixed timeline.

## Real-World Examples with Would: Conditional and Habitual
### Example 1
- Weak: *When I was younger, I am playing outside every evening.*
- Strong: *When I was younger, I would play outside every evening.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *If I had more time, I go to the gym every day.*
- Strong: *If I had more time, I would go to the gym every day.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Would: Conditional and Habitual
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: using present tense after would</summary>

- Weak: *When I was younger, I am playing outside every evening.*
- Strong: *When I was younger, I would play outside every evening.*
- Fix: use the base verb after would (would go, would study, would improve).
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *If I had more time, I go to the gym every day.*
- Strong: *If I had more time, I would go to the gym every day.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in Would: Conditional and Habitual without improving meaning</summary>

- Weak: *The writer attempts Would: Conditional and Habitual, but the sentence becomes difficult to follow due to unnecessary additions.*
- Strong: *The writer uses Would: Conditional and Habitual selectively, so the sentence remains clear and focused.*
- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
</details>
