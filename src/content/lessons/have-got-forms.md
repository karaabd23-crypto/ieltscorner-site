<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Have</strong> is a versatile verb in English, functioning as both a main verb and an auxiliary. Understanding its dual role helps in constructing grammatically correct sentences, especially in expressing possession, forming perfect tenses, and indicating necessity.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She have a car.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She has a car.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Has' is used for third-person singular subjects to indicate possession.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>They has finished their work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>They have finished their work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Have' is used with plural subjects in perfect tenses.</p>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>The verb <strong>'have'</strong> can function as both a main verb and an auxiliary verb. As a main verb, it expresses possession or necessity. As an auxiliary verb, it helps form perfect tenses. Mastering its dual role is crucial at the B2 level for constructing clear and accurate sentences.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing possession or ownership (e.g., "I <strong>have</strong> a car.").</li>
      <li>Indicating necessity (e.g., "I <strong>have</strong> to leave now.").</li>
      <li>Forming perfect tenses (e.g., "She <strong>has</strong> finished her homework.").</li>
      <li>In formal writing and speaking to convey precise meaning.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I <strong>have</strong> a meeting at 3 PM.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>has</strong> completed the report.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">We <strong>have</strong> to submit the form by Friday.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>'have'</strong> as a main verb to show possession.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>'have'</strong> as an auxiliary to form perfect tenses.</li>
      <li>Ensure subject-verb agreement with <strong>'have'</strong> and <strong>'has'</strong>.</li>
      <li>Remember that <strong>'have to'</strong> indicates necessity or obligation.</li>
      <li>In negative and question forms, use 'do not have' or 'does not have'.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Incorrect use of 'have' for possession</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>He have a new laptop.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>He has a new laptop.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'has' for third-person singular subjects.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Incorrect auxiliary use in perfect tense</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She have completed the task.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She has completed the task.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'has' with third-person singular subjects in perfect tenses.</p>
</article>
