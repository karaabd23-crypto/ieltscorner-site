<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead">Task 1 Academic requires precise language to compare data points — not just "more" and "less." Examiners look for a range of comparison structures, accurate proportional language, and correct use of prepositions with numbers.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Weak</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>In 2000 the UK used more energy than France. In 2020 the UK still used more energy. France used less energy than the UK.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Stronger</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The UK consumed roughly twice as much energy as France in 2000, and this gap widened further by 2020, when UK consumption exceeded France's by approximately 40%.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The strong version uses a ratio (twice as much), a trend verb (widened), and a precise approximation (approximately 40%) — all in two sentences instead of three repetitive ones.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Comparison toolkit</p>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Ratio:</strong> twice/three times as much as, half the level of</li>
      <li><strong>Margin:</strong> exceeded X by 20%, higher than X by a factor of two</li>
      <li><strong>Approximation:</strong> approximately, roughly, just under/over, nearly</li>
      <li><strong>Trend:</strong> the gap widened/narrowed, X overtook Y, X converged with Y</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Which structure to use when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Gap is a round multiple: use ratio language (twice as much).</li>
      <li>Gap is a percentage: use "exceeded by" or "higher by".</li>
      <li>Trend changes direction: use overtook, surpassed, converged.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Key patterns</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">X was <strong>approximately twice as high as</strong> Y in [year].</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">X <strong>exceeded</strong> Y <strong>by</strong> roughly 30% by [year].</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The gap between X and Y <strong>narrowed significantly</strong> over the period.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Never write "more higher" or "more faster" — use the comparative alone.</li>
      <li>Use "than" not "then" in comparisons.</li>
      <li>Approximate numbers honestly: "approximately 45%" not "45% exactly" unless the chart shows an exact figure.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Repeating "more than" for every comparison</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>"China used more energy than India. China produced more steel than India. China had more factories than India."</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>"China led India in energy use, steel production, and industrial output across all years shown."</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Consolidate parallel comparisons into one sentence with a list.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Wrong preposition after figures</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>"Sales increased of 20% in Q3."</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>"Sales increased by 20% in Q3."</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "by" for change amounts; "to" for new levels; "at" for rates.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">Lexical Resource accounts for 25% of your Task 1 score. Using only "more than" and "less than" signals a limited range and caps Lexical Resource at Band 5. A varied comparison toolkit — ratios, margins, trend verbs — demonstrates the range examiners need to award Band 7.</p>
