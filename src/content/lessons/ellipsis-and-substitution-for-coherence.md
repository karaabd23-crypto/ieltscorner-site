<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Using Pronouns to Avoid Repetition</strong> helps you maintain clarity and conciseness in your writing by replacing repeated nouns with pronouns. This lesson teaches you how to use pronouns effectively so the reader always knows what each word refers to.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Maria called Maria's manager because Maria needed an update.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Maria called <strong>her</strong> manager because <strong>she</strong> needed an update.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Using 'her' and 'she' replaces the repeated noun 'Maria' for clarity.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The team finished the project, and the project was successful.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The team finished the project, and <strong>it</strong> was successful.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'It' replaces 'the project' to avoid repetition.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Pronouns are words that replace nouns to prevent repetition and maintain sentence fluidity. They help avoid redundancy by substituting repeated nouns with words like 'he', 'she', 'it', 'they', etc. In English, pronouns are essential for clarity, especially at the B2 level, where nuanced understanding of text is required.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>When a noun has been mentioned previously and you want to avoid repeating it.</li>
      <li>In formal writing to maintain clarity and conciseness.</li>
      <li>During spoken responses to keep the conversation smooth and clear.</li>
      <li>When writing essays or reports to enhance readability.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Maria called the manager because <strong>she</strong> needed an update.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The report was long, but <strong>it</strong> was informative.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Ensure each pronoun has a clear antecedent.</li>
      <li>Use pronouns to replace nouns that have been previously mentioned.</li>
      <li>Choose the correct pronoun type: subject, object, possessive, etc.</li>
      <li>Repeat the noun if the pronoun reference is ambiguous.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using a pronoun with more than one possible reference</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>When Sara met Lina, <strong>she</strong> looked worried.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>When Sara met Lina, <strong>Lina</strong> looked worried.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> repeat the noun if the pronoun could point to two different people.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>choosing the wrong pronoun form after a preposition or verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The teacher spoke to <strong>he</strong> after class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The teacher spoke to <strong>him</strong> after class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> check whether the sentence needs a subject, object, or possessive pronoun.</p>
</article>
</div>
