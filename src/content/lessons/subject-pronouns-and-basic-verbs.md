## Real-World Examples with Subject Pronouns and Basic Verbs
### Example 1
- Weak: *When Maria met Ana, she said she needed help.*
- Strong: *When Maria met Ana, Maria said she needed help.*
- Why it works: the grammar choice supports communication instead of confusing the reader.

### Example 2
- Weak: *The managers told the interns that they were unprepared.*
- Strong: *The managers told the interns that the report was unprepared.*
- Why it works: the reader can follow the logic without re-reading.

## Common Errors with Subject Pronouns and Basic Verbs
<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 1: using unclear pronoun references in complex sentences</summary>

- Weak: *When Maria met Ana, she said she needed help.*
- Strong: *When Maria met Ana, Maria said she needed help.*
- Fix: repeat the noun when a pronoun could refer to more than one person or thing.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 2: using the right idea with unstable sentence structure</summary>

- Weak: *The managers told the interns that they were unprepared.*
- Strong: *The managers told the interns that the report was unprepared.*
- Fix: revise agreement and clause structure so the form supports meaning.
</details>

<details class="lesson-accordion lesson-error">
<summary>Error 3: adding complexity in Subject Pronouns and Basic Verbs without improving meaning</summary>

- Fix: keep only structures that improve precision or clarity.
