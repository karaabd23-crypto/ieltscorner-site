<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Apostrophe and Possession</strong> is crucial for showing ownership and forming contractions in English. Mastering this small but powerful punctuation mark can significantly enhance the clarity and professionalism of your writing.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The students books were left on the back table.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The students' books were left on the back table.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The apostrophe after 'students' indicates that the books belong to the students.</p>

  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>It's important that the team checks every heading.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"It's" is the contraction for "it is," indicating the correct form here.</p>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>An apostrophe is a punctuation mark used to indicate possession or to form contractions by replacing omitted letters.</p>
    <p>For possession, use <strong>'s</strong> with singular nouns (e.g., the manager's report) and <strong>s'</strong> with plural nouns ending in 's' (e.g., the managers' meeting).</p>
    <p>In contractions, apostrophes replace missing letters (e.g., it's for it is). Distinguish between <strong>its</strong> (possessive) and <strong>it's</strong> (it is).</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering these distinctions enhances clarity and precision in writing.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Indicating ownership by a singular noun or a plural noun ending in 's'.</li>
      <li>Forming contractions to replace missing letters.</li>
      <li>Writing formal and informal texts where clarity is essential.</li>
      <li>Editing your writing to ensure correct punctuation usage.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The <strong>director's</strong> decision was final.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The <strong>teachers'</strong> lounge is newly renovated.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>It's</strong> important to review your work carefully.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 's for singular possession.</li>
      <li>Use s' for plural possession when the noun ends in 's'.</li>
      <li>Never use apostrophes for regular plurals.</li>
      <li>Differentiate between its (possessive) and it's (it is).</li>
      <li>Double-check if the apostrophe indicates possession or a contraction.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Adding apostrophes to plural nouns that are not possessive</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The teacher collected three essay's before lunch.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The teacher collected three essays before lunch.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Do not use apostrophes for simple plurals.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Mixing up its and it's</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The company changed it's policy last month.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The company changed its policy last month.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "it's" only for "it is" or "it has"; use "its" for possession.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Choosing the wrong apostrophe form for plural ownership</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The students's projects were displayed in the hall.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The students' projects were displayed in the hall.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> For plural owners ending in 's', place the apostrophe after 's'.</p>
</article>
</div>
