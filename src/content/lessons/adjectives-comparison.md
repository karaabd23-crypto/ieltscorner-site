<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Adjectives in Comparison</strong> help you compare people, things, and ideas clearly. English uses special forms for <em>more</em>, <em>less</em>, and <em>the most</em>. In this lesson, you will learn how to compare without mixing the patterns, ensuring your sentences are grammatically correct and convey the intended meaning.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Adjectives in comparison are used to describe differences in the qualities or quantities of nouns. They help indicate whether one thing is superior, inferior, or equal to another.</p>
    <p>Comparative adjectives are formed by adding <strong>-er</strong> to one-syllable adjectives or using <strong>more</strong> with longer adjectives. Superlatives are formed by adding <strong>-est</strong> or using <strong>most</strong>.</p>
    <p>This skill is crucial at the B2 level for crafting precise and varied sentences in both spoken and written English.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Comparing two items: use comparatives like "cheaper" or "more expensive".</li>
      <li>Identifying the highest or lowest degree among three or more items: use superlatives like "cheapest" or "most expensive".</li>
      <li>Expressing equality between two items: use "as...as".</li>
      <li>Writing essays or reports where comparison is necessary.</li>
      <li>Engaging in discussions where you need to compare options or ideas.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">This laptop is <strong>faster</strong> than the previous model.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She is the <strong>most talented</strong> artist in the group.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The project is <strong>as important as</strong> the budget allows.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>-er</strong> for one-syllable adjectives and <strong>more</strong> for longer adjectives when comparing two items.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>-est</strong> for one-syllable adjectives and <strong>most</strong> for longer adjectives when identifying the highest degree.</li>
      <li>Do not mix <strong>-er</strong> with <strong>more</strong> or <strong>-est</strong> with <strong>most</strong>.</li>
      <li>Use "as...as" to show equality between two things.</li>
      <li>Always use "than" after comparatives and "in/of" after superlatives.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Combining comparison forms incorrectly</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This phone is more better than my old one.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>This phone is better than my old one.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use either "better" or "more" but not both.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using double comparatives</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>This route is more faster during rush hour.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>This route is faster during rush hour.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use one comparative marker only, such as "faster."</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Using "than" after a superlative</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>It is the cheapest than the old model.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>It is cheaper than the old model.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use "than" with comparatives and "in/of" with superlatives.</p>
</article>
</div>
