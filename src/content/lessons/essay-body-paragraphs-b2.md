<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Essay Structure: Body Paragraphs</strong> is where your score is usually won or lost. A good body paragraph has one main idea, clear explanation, and one useful example or result. In this lesson, you will learn how to build a paragraph that stays focused from start to finish.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>This approach seems useful because it helps people in several ways.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>A major reason to improve public transport is that it increases access for workers who cannot afford private cars.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger sentence gives the paragraph a clear direction.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>For example, one local program made services better for residents.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>For example, when one district expanded bus frequency, commute delays fell and attendance improved among shift workers.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The better version develops the idea instead of circling around it.</p>
</article>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>This lesson focuses on one writing move that changes clarity and score at the same time.</p>
<p>Body paragraphs are where scores are won. Each paragraph should do one clear job: state a main point, explain it, and support it. When paragraphs combine too many reasons or move away from the thesis, the essay loses coherence even if the grammar is accurate.</p>
<p>Use this structure in IELTS and CELPIP essays whenever you need to develop a reason or viewpoint. Topic sentences should carry the main point, and supporting sentences should stay attached to that same idea.</p>
<p>At B2 level, stronger writing comes from better paragraph control, not from simply making the language longer.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul><li>Start by identifying the exact job of the task or paragraph.</li><li>Keep support specific enough to prove the point rather than decorate it.</li><li>Check that every sentence moves the response toward its purpose.</li></ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack"><div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">A major reason to improve public transport is that it increases access for workers who cannot afford private cars.</div>
<div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">For example, when one district expanded bus frequency, commute delays fell and attendance improved among shift workers.</div></div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul><li>Write a topic sentence that states one main reason.</li><li>Explain why that reason matters in one or two sentences.</li><li>Add a concrete example or realistic consequence.</li><li>Close the paragraph by linking the evidence back to the main point.</li><li>Cut any sentence that introduces a new reason.</li></ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>writing a body paragraph without a clear topic sentence</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>There are many things to consider. People have different views. Some agree, others do not.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>One reason people prefer online learning is the flexibility it offers.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Begin every body paragraph with one sentence that states its single main idea.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>adding a supporting point without an explanation or example</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Online learning is flexible. Many people use it.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Online learning is flexible: students can pause and replay lectures, which suits different learning speeds.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Follow each supporting point with a because, for example, or consequence explanation.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>introducing a new topic inside a paragraph that already has one</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Online learning is flexible. Also, it is cheaper. And teachers can use technology.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Online learning is flexible and cheaper than traditional courses, making it accessible to more students.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Keep each paragraph focused on one idea — move extra points to a new paragraph.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance"><strong>&#127919; Why it matters:</strong> strong ideas do not help enough if the writing skill is weak. In IELTS and CELPIP, this skill helps the examiner follow your position, support, and logic without extra work. That usually means a cleaner, stronger score.</p>
