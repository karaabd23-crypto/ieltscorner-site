<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Prepositions of Place</strong> are essential for describing the location of objects and people in English. Mastering these prepositions helps make your sentences clear and precise, which is crucial for effective communication in both written and spoken contexts.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The cat is in the table.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The cat is on the table.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use <strong>on</strong> for surfaces, not <strong>in</strong>.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She lives on a small town.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She lives in a small town.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use <strong>in</strong> for towns and cities, not <strong>on</strong>.</p>
</article>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Prepositions of place are words that describe the location or position of something in relation to other objects. They help clarify where something is situated in space.</p>
    <p>Common prepositions of place include <strong>in</strong>, <strong>on</strong>, <strong>at</strong>, <strong>under</strong>, <strong>between</strong>, and <strong>next to</strong>.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, understanding these prepositions allows for more precise descriptions in both written and spoken communication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Describing the location of objects in a room or space.</li>
      <li>Giving directions or explaining where something is situated.</li>
      <li>Writing or speaking about spatial relationships in essays or presentations.</li>
      <li>Answering questions about where events or actions take place.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The book is <strong>on</strong> the table.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She sat <strong>between</strong> her friends.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The keys are <strong>under</strong> the mat.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>in</strong> for enclosed spaces (e.g., in the room).</li>
      <li>Use <strong>on</strong> for surfaces (e.g., on the wall).</li>
      <li>Use <strong>at</strong> for specific points (e.g., at the door).</li>
      <li>Remember that some prepositions are idiomatic and must be memorized.</li>
      <li>Visualize the spatial relationship to choose the correct preposition.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using the wrong preposition for surfaces</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The picture is in the wall.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The picture is on the wall.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use <strong>on</strong> for surfaces.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>confusing enclosed spaces with surfaces</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She is on the room.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She is in the room.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use <strong>in</strong> for enclosed spaces.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using incorrect prepositions for points</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Meet me in the entrance.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Meet me at the entrance.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use <strong>at</strong> for specific points.</p>
</article>
</div>
