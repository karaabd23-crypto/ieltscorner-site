<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Contrast</strong> transitions are essential for expressing differences clearly in English. This lesson focuses on using words and phrases that effectively highlight contrasts in writing and speaking, such as 'however' and 'on the other hand'.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>The route was cheaper. Also, it took longer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The route was cheaper; <strong>however</strong>, it took longer.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger version clearly shows the contrast between cost and time.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>The team worked hard. But they did not meet the deadline.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The team worked hard; <strong>nevertheless</strong>, they did not meet the deadline.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger version highlights the unexpected outcome despite the effort.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Contrast transition words and phrases are used to show differences or oppositions between ideas or statements. They help clarify how two or more ideas differ from each other in a sentence or paragraph.</p>
    <p>Common contrast transitions include words like <strong>however</strong>, <strong>on the other hand</strong>, and <strong>although</strong>. These are important at a B2 level as they enhance clarity and coherence in writing and speaking.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing a difference between two ideas in academic writing.</li>
      <li>Highlighting opposing viewpoints in an essay or report.</li>
      <li>Clarifying contradictions in spoken responses during exams.</li>
      <li>Writing professional emails where contrasting information needs to be clearly communicated.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>However</strong>, the project was completed on time despite the initial delays.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The new policy is beneficial; <strong>on the other hand</strong>, it may increase costs.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use contrast words to show differences, not similarities.</li>
      <li>Ensure the contrast word matches the logical relationship between ideas.</li>
      <li>Avoid overusing contrast words in the same paragraph.</li>
      <li>Place contrast words at the beginning of a sentence for emphasis.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using a linker that shows the wrong relationship</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The route became faster; for example, more workers used it.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The route became faster; <strong>however</strong>, more workers used it.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> choose the linker from the real logic between the ideas</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>repeating the same linker in every sentence</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span><strong>However</strong>, the buses were late. <strong>However</strong>, the station was crowded.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span><strong>However</strong>, the buses were late. <strong>On the other hand</strong>, the station was crowded.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> vary linkers when the relationship changes</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using a contrast linker when not needed</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The weather was sunny. <strong>However</strong>, we went to the park.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The weather was sunny, so we went to the park.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> only use contrast linkers when there is a genuine contrast</p>
</article>
</div>
