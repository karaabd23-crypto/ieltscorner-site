<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Phrasal Verbs</strong> are essential for making your English sound natural and fluent. They often change the meaning of the base verb, so understanding them is crucial for effective communication. This lesson will guide you through their formation, provide clear examples, and highlight common mistakes to avoid.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>The meeting was putted off until Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The meeting was put off until Friday.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The verb 'put' does not change form in this phrasal verb; only the particle 'off' is added.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>Students need to find why the answer is wrong out.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Students need to find out why the answer is wrong.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Find out' is a phrasal verb meaning to discover or learn information.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Phrasal verbs combine a base verb with one or more particles (e.g., up, off, out) to create a new meaning distinct from the original verb. Understanding phrasal verbs is essential for achieving fluency and naturalness in English communication.</p>
    <p>At the B2 level, mastering phrasal verbs helps in both spoken and written contexts, enhancing clarity and expressiveness.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Expressing actions or states in a more idiomatic way.</li>
      <li>Incorporating informal or conversational tone in speech or writing.</li>
      <li>Understanding and using English in real-life contexts, such as media or conversations.</li>
      <li>Improving comprehension of native speakers who frequently use phrasal verbs.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The manager <strong>called off</strong> the meeting due to unforeseen circumstances.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She needs to <strong>look up</strong> the information before the presentation.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Learn phrasal verbs as a complete unit, not as separate words.</li>
      <li>Check if the object can be placed between the verb and particle.</li>
      <li>Use phrasal verbs in informal contexts; opt for formal verbs in formal writing.</li>
      <li>Be aware of phrasal verbs with multiple meanings and use context to determine the correct one.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>breaking the phrasal verb form or word order</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Please look the word up in your notes before class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Please look up the word in your notes before class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Ensure the particle follows the verb when no object intervenes.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>putting the object in the wrong place</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Please look the number up before class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Please look up the number before class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Place the object after the particle if it is not a pronoun.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>treating the phrasal verb like an ordinary past tense form</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The event was calleded off because of snow.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The event was called off because of snow.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Conjugate only the verb, not the particle.</p>
</article>
</div>
