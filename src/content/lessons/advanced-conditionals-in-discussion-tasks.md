<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Advanced Conditional Sentences for Effective Discussions</strong> involve using complex conditional structures to articulate hypothetical, likely, or unreal scenarios. Mastering these sentences enables clear communication of potential outcomes in various contexts.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>If I will see him, I tell him the news.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>If I see him, I will tell him the news.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use the present simple in the if-clause for likely future results.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>If she had known, she will have acted differently.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>If she had known, she would have acted differently.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Use "would have" for unreal past conditions and results.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Advanced conditional sentences are used to express complex ideas about hypothetical, likely, or unreal situations. They connect a condition with its result, helping to articulate potential outcomes and dependencies. At the B2 level, mastering these sentences enhances clarity and precision in communication.</p>
    <p>Formation patterns include a variety of tenses, such as past perfect for unreal past conditions and present simple for likely future conditions.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Discussing hypothetical scenarios in professional meetings or academic debates.</li>
      <li>Explaining potential outcomes in essays or reports.</li>
      <li>Planning projects where outcomes depend on specific conditions.</li>
      <li>Engaging in discussions that require analyzing different possibilities.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">If the team <strong>had finished</strong> the project earlier, they <strong>would have saved</strong> costs.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">If we <strong>invest</strong> in new resources, our efficiency <strong>will improve</strong>.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use past perfect in the if-clause for unreal past conditions.</li>
      <li>Present simple in the if-clause indicates likely future results.</li>
      <li>Avoid "will" in the if-clause unless expressing willingness or intention.</li>
      <li>Ensure logical alignment of verb tenses in both clauses.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using "will" in the if-clause</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>If he will call, I will be ready.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>If he calls, I will be ready.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use the present simple in the if-clause for future conditions.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>mixing real and unreal conditionals</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>If I knew the answer, I will tell you.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>If I knew the answer, I would tell you.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Keep the entire sentence in the same conditional form.</p>
</article>
</div>
