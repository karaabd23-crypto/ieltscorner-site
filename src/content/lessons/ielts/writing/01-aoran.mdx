## What is this entry doing here?

In *A–Z* grammar books, some headwords look weird (like **aoran**) because they function as **index-like hooks**:
they catch spelling/word-form questions learners actually type or search for.

In IELTS writing, this matters because *accuracy is often lost at the spelling + form level*, not at the "big grammar" level.

<Callout type="tip" title="IELTS-writing connection">
If you can't reliably spell or recognize a word form, you're more likely to avoid it — which shrinks your lexical range and makes your writing repetitive.
</Callout>

## The skill you're building

### 1) Form-checking habit
When you meet an unfamiliar word:
- check spelling
- check word class (noun/verb/adjective)
- check typical collocations (what it "likes" to sit next to)

### 2) Replacement strategy (Band 7+)
If a word is risky, don't downgrade to a childish synonym. Swap to a safer *academic* alternative.

## Practical micro-drill (30 seconds)

Write 2 sentences using a "safe academic substitute":

- The evidence is ________, so the conclusion is premature.
- The term is ________, which causes confusion in public debates.
