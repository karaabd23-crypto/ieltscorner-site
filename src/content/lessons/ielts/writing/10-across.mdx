## What across means

**Across** helps you show range or distribution. It is useful when one idea is true in many places, groups, or fields.

In IELTS writing, this word is common in Task 1 reports and in Task 2 arguments. It sounds natural when you want to connect many categories in one sentence.

<div class="lesson-grid">
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Range idea</h3>
    <p>Internet use rose across all age groups.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Distribution idea</h3>
    <p>Support was strong across rural and urban areas.</p>
  </div>
</div>

## Useful patterns

<PatternTable
  caption="High-value patterns"
  rows={[
    { pattern: "across + plural noun", meaning: "many groups/places", example: "Prices increased across major cities." },
    { pattern: "across + all + plural noun", meaning: "full range", example: "The trend appeared across all sectors." },
    { pattern: "across + A and B", meaning: "two category groups", example: "The policy was accepted across schools and workplaces." }
  ]}
/>

<details class="panel">
  <summary><strong>Across vs in</strong></summary>
  <p>Use <strong>in</strong> for one place (in Canada). Use <strong>across</strong> for many places or groups (across Canada, across regions).</p>
</details>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Watch out">
- "across the country" ✅ but "in across the country" ❌  
- Using across with one person or one item (usually unnatural).  
- Repeating across too often in one paragraph.
</Callout>

## Practice

1) Rewrite: *The change happened in many sectors.*  
2) Fix: *The trend increased in across regions.*  
3) Write one sentence with *across all age groups*.
