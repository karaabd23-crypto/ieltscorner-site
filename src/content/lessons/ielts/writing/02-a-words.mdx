## Key idea

Many **a-words** (like *asleep, awake, afloat, abroad, alone, ajar*) behave like **predicative adjectives**:
they usually appear **after a verb** (especially *be*, *seem*, *feel*, *remain*), not directly before a noun.

<Callout type="note" title="Predicative adjective = sits after the verb">
He is <strong>asleep</strong>. / The door is <strong>ajar</strong>.
</Callout>

## IELTS Writing upgrade

Instead of basic adjectives, a-words can add precision (when correct):

- Basic: *The door was open.*
- Better: *The door was **ajar**.* (slightly open)

- Basic: *He was sleeping.*
- Better: *He was **asleep**.* (state)

<Callout type="warning" title="Typical IELTS mistake">
❌ "an asleep child" (usually wrong)  
✅ "a sleeping child" OR "the child was asleep"
</Callout>

## Quick practice

Rewrite using an a-word (if possible):

1) The passengers were sleeping.  
2) The door was slightly open.  
3) Many elderly people live by themselves.
