## Why this language is useful

In IELTS writing, you often accept one idea and refuse another. Good writers do this politely and directly.

If your language is too soft, your opinion is weak. If your language is too strong, it sounds aggressive. The goal is clear and respectful tone.

<div class="lesson-grid">
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Accepting</h3>

  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Refusing</h3>
    <p>However, I do not agree that cars should be banned completely.</p>

## Useful patterns

<PatternTable
  caption="Polite and clear sentence frames"
  rows={[
    { pattern: "I agree that + clause", meaning: "accept an idea", example: "I agree that schools need more digital tools." },
    { pattern: "I do not agree that + clause", meaning: "refuse an idea politely", example: "I do not agree that exams should be removed." },
    { pattern: "While I accept X, I disagree with Y", meaning: "balanced opinion", example: "While I accept online learning, I disagree with fully remote schooling." }
  ]}
/>

<details class="panel">
  <summary><strong>Use this in IELTS Task 2</strong></summary>
  <p>In your introduction, state your main position clearly. In body paragraphs, accept one point and then explain why you reject another point.</p>
</details>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid these">
- Saying "I am not agree" (wrong form).  
- Being too emotional instead of logical.  
- Refusing an idea without giving a reason.
</Callout>

## Practice

1) Fix: *I am not agree with this policy.*  
2) Add one reason: *I disagree that homework should be removed.*  
3) Write one sentence with *While I accept...*.
