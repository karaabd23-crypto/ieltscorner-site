## What these words mean

**Above** means at a higher level. **Below** means at a lower level. In normal IELTS Task 2 writing, you often do not need these words for reference.

In reports or long documents, writers use phrases like *the figure above* or *the table below*. In IELTS essays, it is usually better to repeat the idea clearly instead of saying *as mentioned above*.

<div class="lesson-grid">
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Use in reports</h3>
    <p>The chart below shows a clear increase in costs.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>Use in essays</h3>
    <p>As discussed earlier, online study helps many workers.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout type="tip" title="Simple IELTS rule">
In Task 2, avoid "as stated above" unless you really need it. Repeat the key idea in a short, clear sentence.
</Callout>

## Useful patterns

<PatternTable
  caption="Clear patterns you can copy"
  rows={[
    { pattern: "above + noun", meaning: "higher than something", example: "Temperatures stayed above 30°C all week." },
    { pattern: "below + noun", meaning: "lower than something", example: "Income was below the national average." },
    { pattern: "as discussed earlier", meaning: "refer back in essays", example: "As discussed earlier, public transport reduces traffic." }
  ]}
/>

<details class="panel">
  <summary><strong>Why this matters for IELTS</strong></summary>
  <p>Clear repetition is better than unclear referencing. The examiner should understand your point in one reading.</p>
</details>

## Common mistakes

<Callout type="warning" title="Avoid these errors">
- "As said above" in a short essay where nothing is clearly referenced.  
- Using above/below when you really mean more/less.  
- Writing very long reference phrases that add no new meaning.
</Callout>

## Practice

1) Rewrite: *As mentioned above, this issue is serious.*  
2) Rewrite: *The number is above than last year.*  
3) Rewrite with **below**: *The score is lower than 6.0.*
