## The core meaning

- **a bit** = slightly / to a small degree (often modifies adjectives/adverbs)
- **a bit of** = a small amount of + noun

<div class="lesson-grid">
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>a bit + adjective/adverb</h3>
    <p>It is <strong>a bit</strong> expensive.</p>
    <p>She spoke <strong>a bit</strong> too quickly.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="panel">
    <h3>a bit of + noun</h3>
    <p>We need <strong>a bit of</strong> evidence.</p>
    <p>There was <strong>a bit of</strong> confusion.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<Callout type="warning" title="IELTS Writing trap">
Overusing "a bit" makes your writing sound hesitant and imprecise.  
Band 7+ writers soften claims with <strong>measured language</strong>, not vague filler.
</Callout>

## Better academic alternatives

Use these when you want "a bit" but in a more academic tone:

<PatternTable
  caption="Upgrade options"
  rows={[
    { pattern: "slightly / somewhat + adj", meaning: "mild degree (academic)", example: "The results were somewhat inconsistent." },
    { pattern: "to a limited extent", meaning: "controlled limitation", example: "This policy worked to a limited extent." },
    { pattern: "a modest amount of + noun", meaning: "small quantity (formal)", example: "A modest amount of funding improved access." }
  ]}
/>

## Practice: rewrite (Band 7+ tone)

Rewrite more academically:

1) It is a bit hard to measure.  
2) There is a bit of pollution in cities.  
3) Some people are a bit worried.
