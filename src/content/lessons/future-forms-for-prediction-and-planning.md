<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Using Future Tenses: Predictions and Plans</strong> helps you express future actions with clarity. Selecting the right future tense ensures your listener or reader understands your intentions, predictions, or scheduled events accurately. This lesson guides you in choosing the appropriate future form based on context.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>She will going to the meeting tomorrow.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She <strong>is going</strong> to the meeting tomorrow.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The present continuous is used for scheduled future events.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Incorrect</span>They will going to launch the product next week.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>They <strong>are going to launch</strong> the product next week.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">'Going to' is used for planned actions.</p>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Future tenses express actions or events that will occur after the present moment. They are essential for making predictions, outlining plans, and describing scheduled events.</p>
    <p>The main future forms include 'will' for predictions and spontaneous decisions, 'going to' for premeditated plans or intentions, and the present continuous for definite future arrangements.</p>
    <p>Mastering these forms at the B2 level is crucial for effective communication about future events.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Predicting future events based on evidence or opinion.</li>
      <li>Describing plans or intentions that have been decided beforehand.</li>
      <li>Discussing scheduled events or timetables.</li>
      <li>Making decisions at the moment of speaking.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I <strong>will visit</strong> my family next weekend.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She <strong>is going to start</strong> a new job next month.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The train <strong>leaves</strong> at 6 PM tomorrow.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use 'will' for predictions and spontaneous decisions.</li>
      <li>Use 'going to' for planned actions or intentions.</li>
      <li>Use present continuous for scheduled events.</li>
      <li>Ensure the verb form matches the intended future use.</li>
      <li>Avoid mixing future forms without a clear reason.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Confusing 'will' and 'going to'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>She will going to start a new project.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>She <strong>is going to start</strong> a new project.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'going to' for plans or intentions.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Using 'will' for scheduled events</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The train will leave at 5 PM.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The train <strong>leaves</strong> at 5 PM.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use present continuous for scheduled events.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>Overusing 'will' for all future contexts</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>We will have a meeting tomorrow at 10 AM.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>We <strong>are having</strong> a meeting tomorrow at 10 AM.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use present continuous for fixed future arrangements.</p>
</article>
</div>
