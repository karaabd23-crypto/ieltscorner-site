## Examples
<p class="lesson-section-lede">&#10024; <strong>Read the examples first.</strong> They show the pattern for <u>Business Vocabulary</u> in real sentences.</p>
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>The company helped workers do more work.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>The company improved productivity through a targeted training program.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The stronger version names the real meaning instead of staying vague.</p>

  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>They expanded recruitment after turnover increased.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">The better sentence sounds more natural for a real task and a real reader.</p>

## Core Lesson
<p class="lesson-section-lede">&#129517; <strong>This is the main explanation.</strong> Read it once for meaning first. Then use the boxes as a quick guide.</p>
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Core idea</p>
    <p>This lesson helps you say the same idea with sharper, more natural vocabulary.</p>
<p>Business Vocabulary teaches useful words for training, hiring, productivity, and workplace development.</p>
<p>Use this vocabulary when describing jobs, companies, staff development, and performance.</p>
<p>At B1 level, learn fewer words but learn them well: meaning, collocation, and one model sentence.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it here</p>
    <ul><li>Choose words that match the stage: hiring, training, output, or promotion.</li><li>Pair the word with a realistic workplace action or result.</li><li>Keep the tone clear and practical instead of sounding like a slogan.</li></ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Watch it work</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack"><div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">Regular training programs improved staff productivity.</div>
<div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The company expanded recruitment after reducing turnover.</div></div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Remember this</p>
    <ul><li>Choose the meaning first before you choose the word.</li><li>Learn the word with a natural collocation or partner phrase.</li><li>Use the new word in one short sentence right away.</li><li>Replace vague words only when the new word stays accurate.</li><li>Keep the register stable so the language fits the task.</li></ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<p class="lesson-section-lede">&#9888;&#65039; <strong>These are the mistakes learners make most often.</strong> Check the weak sentence, then notice the small change that fixes it.</p>
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>using broad workplace words instead of the exact business term</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The office did things to help staff.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The office launched a staff training program.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> name the exact business action or process</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>using a work term without the result attached</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Productivity changed after the workshops.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Productivity improved after the workshops.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> add the real workplace result to the topic word</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>treating work vocabulary like isolated labels</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Recruitment, promotion, workload.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>A lighter workload and clearer promotion paths improved retention.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> connect the workplace words inside one real sentence</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance"><strong>&#127919; Why it matters:</strong> good vocabulary is not just about knowing a word. You need the <em>right</em> word in the <em>right</em> sentence. When you control Business Vocabulary, you sound clearer, more natural, and more precise in real English.</p>
