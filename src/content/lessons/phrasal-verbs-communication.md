<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Phrasal Verbs: Communication</strong> focuses on essential verb + particle combinations used in everyday communication. Mastering these will help you express ideas more naturally and effectively in English conversations, whether in meetings, casual talks, or written correspondence.</p></div>

## Examples
<div class="lesson-example-grid">
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 1</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>He mentioned the new policy in the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>He <strong>brought up</strong> the new policy in the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">Using "brought up" clearly indicates the introduction of a topic.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-example-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Example 2</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Too weak</span>She searched the word in the dictionary.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>She <strong>looked up</strong> the word in the dictionary.</p>
  <p class="lesson-card-note">"Looked up" is a more precise phrasal verb for searching information.</p>
</article>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Phrasal verbs are combinations of verbs and particles (prepositions or adverbs) that create a meaning different from the original verb. In the context of communication, these verbs help convey specific actions or nuances in interactions, such as introducing topics or clarifying information.</p>
    <p>For B2 level learners, mastering these phrasal verbs enhances both spoken and written communication by making expressions more natural and precise.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Introducing a topic in a conversation or meeting (e.g., "bring up").</li>
      <li>Searching for information (e.g., "look up").</li>
      <li>Clarifying or expanding on a point (e.g., "spell out").</li>
      <li>Ending a conversation or wrapping up a discussion (e.g., "wind up").</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Bring up</strong> the new policy during the meeting to get feedback.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">I need to <strong>look up</strong> the details before I can confirm.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">She had to <strong>spell out</strong> the instructions for everyone to understand.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Learn phrasal verbs as fixed expressions for specific contexts.</li>
      <li>Check if the object can be placed between the verb and particle.</li>
      <li>Use phrasal verbs in informal and semi-formal communication settings.</li>
      <li>Ensure the phrasal verb fits the intended meaning and context.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>choosing the wrong particle with a familiar verb</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The manager pointed the delay out about at the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The manager <strong>pointed out</strong> the delay at the meeting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> learn the full phrasal verb as one item</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>placing the object in an unnatural position</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Please look the timetable up before class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Please <strong>look up</strong> the timetable before class.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> check where the object belongs in the phrase</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using a phrasal verb without understanding its meaning</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The team carried out late because of traffic.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The team <strong>carried out</strong> the safety check despite the traffic delay.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> use the phrasal verb only when you know what action it describes</p>
</article>
</div>
