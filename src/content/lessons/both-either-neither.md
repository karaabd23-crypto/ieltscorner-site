<div class="lesson-context"><p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Both, Either, Neither</strong> helps you talk about two people, two things, or two choices clearly. These words look simple, but each one gives a different meaning. In this lesson, you will learn how to choose the right one and keep the sentence natural.</p></div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p><strong>Both</strong>, <strong>Either</strong>, and <strong>Neither</strong> are correlative conjunctions used to connect two elements in a sentence. <strong>Both</strong> includes two items, <strong>Either</strong> suggests one or the other, and <strong>Neither</strong> excludes both. These distinctions enhance clarity and precision in communication, crucial for B2 level learners.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Connecting two subjects or objects in a sentence.</li>
      <li>Clarifying choices in decision-making contexts.</li>
      <li>Balancing options in formal writing or speech.</li>
      <li>Ensuring verb agreement with the subject closest to the verb in either/or and neither/nor constructions.</li>
      <li>Expressing inclusion or exclusion in comparisons.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Both</strong> the manager <strong>and</strong> the staff approved the plan.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Either</strong> the library <strong>or</strong> the classrooms are open this evening.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence"><strong>Neither</strong> the buses <strong>nor</strong> the train service was reliable today.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Use <strong>both</strong> to include two items or people.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>either</strong> to indicate one or the other of two options.</li>
      <li>Use <strong>neither</strong> to exclude both options.</li>
      <li>Ensure verb agreement with the subject closest to the verb in either/or and neither/nor constructions.</li>
      <li>Maintain parallel structure when using these conjunctions.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>breaking parallel structure or agreement in a correlative pair</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Neither the buses nor the train service were reliable today.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Neither the buses nor the train service was reliable today.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Ensure verb agreement with the subject closest to the verb.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>breaking the pair with unmatched grammar</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Both to revise grammar and vocabulary practice are useful.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Both revising grammar and practising vocabulary are useful.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Keep both sides of the pair in the same grammatical form.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 3</p>
  <h3>using a singular verb with a plural nearer subject</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>Neither the principal nor the teachers was happy with the change.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>Neither the principal nor the teachers were happy with the change.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Check the subject closest to the verb for correct agreement.</p>
</article>
</div>
