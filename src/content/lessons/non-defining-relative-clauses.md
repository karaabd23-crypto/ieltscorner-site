<div class="lesson-context">
  <p class="lesson-context-lead"><strong>Non-defining relative clauses</strong> allow you to add supplementary information to a sentence, enhancing its richness without altering the core meaning. This lesson will guide you in effectively using these clauses to add depth and clarity to your writing.</p>
</div>

## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-core">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Meaning</p>
    <p>Non-defining relative clauses provide additional information about a noun without altering the main meaning of the sentence. They are set off by commas and use relative pronouns like <strong>who</strong>, <strong>which</strong>, and <strong>whose</strong>.</p>
    <p>This structure is crucial at the B2 level for adding depth and detail to your writing, enhancing clarity and sophistication.</p>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-when">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Use it when</p>
    <ul>
      <li>You want to add non-essential information about a noun in a sentence.</li>
      <li>Writing formal texts where additional context is beneficial.</li>
      <li>Describing a noun that is already clearly identified.</li>
      <li>Providing extra details in academic writing or reports.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-pattern">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">See it</p>
    <div class="lesson-pattern-stack">
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">My brother, <strong>who lives in New York</strong>, is visiting us next week.</div>
      <div class="lesson-pattern-sentence">The book, <strong>which was published last year</strong>, has become a bestseller.</div>
    </div>
  </section>
  <section class="lesson-panel lesson-panel-remember">
    <p class="lesson-panel-label">Quick rules</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Always use commas to set off non-defining relative clauses.</li>
      <li>Do not use 'that' in non-defining relative clauses.</li>
      <li>Ensure the clause provides additional, non-essential information.</li>
      <li>Use 'who' for people, 'which' for things, and 'whose' for possession.</li>
    </ul>
  </section>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 1</p>
  <h3>Using 'that' instead of 'which' or 'who'</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>The book, that was on the table, is mine.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>The book, <strong>which was on the table</strong>, is mine.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Use 'which' for things and 'who' for people in non-defining clauses.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Common problem 2</p>
  <h3>Omitting commas around the clause</h3>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Weak</span>My sister who lives in London is visiting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Strong</span>My sister, <strong>who lives in London</strong>, is visiting.</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Always use commas to enclose non-defining clauses.</p>
</article>
</div>
