## How It Works
<div class="lesson-teach-grid">
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Verb check first</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Read each verb and confirm tense is consistent and correct</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Underline every verb in the response; verify each against the time signal</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Article pass</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Check "a/an/the" before nouns; remove or add as needed</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">"I need the advice" → "I need advice"; "She is a engineer" → "an engineer"</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Bullet point audit</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Return to the prompt and confirm every required point is addressed</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Tick off each instruction or bullet after finding it in your response</p>
</div>
<div class="lesson-teach-panel">
  <p class="lesson-teach-pattern">Word count estimate</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-meaning">Confirm Task 2 response is not significantly under the guideline</p>
  <p class="lesson-teach-example">Count paragraphs and estimate words; add a sentence if a section is thin</p>
</div>
</div>

## Common Mistakes
<div class="lesson-error-grid">
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Reading for meaning, not error</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Reading your own response and thinking "it sounds good"</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Reading for one error type at a time (verbs, then articles, then bullet coverage)</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> The brain auto-corrects familiar text. Targeting one feature at a time defeats this habit.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">Skipping the prompt on revision</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Only re-reading your response, not comparing it to the prompt</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Checking your response against each bullet point in the prompt</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> Omitting a bullet costs Task Fulfilment marks that no grammar fix can recover.</p>
</article>
<article class="lesson-error-card">
  <p class="lesson-card-label">No word count check</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-weak"><span>Avoid</span>Finishing Task 2 at 80 words without noticing it is significantly short</p>
  <p class="lesson-line lesson-line-strong"><span>Better</span>Estimating word count during revision and adding a sentence if under target</p>
  <p class="lesson-fix-line"><strong>Fix:</strong> CELPIP Task 2 responses under 150 words rarely score CLB 9 on Content.</p>
</article>
</div>

## Why It Matters
<p class="lesson-importance">CELPIP Linguistic Accuracy at CLB 9 requires consistent control with only minor errors. Candidates who draft fluently but skip revision consistently leave correctable errors in their response. A two-minute sequenced revision routine (verbs, articles, bullet audit) converts draft-quality writing into exam-quality writing at the cost of 120 seconds.</p>
